## Converting Fractions, Decimals and Percents

**TEAS objective M.1.1** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can move quickly between fractions, decimals and percents, including mixed numbers.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it to divide a fraction's top by its bottom.

---

### 1. The conversions you need

| From → To | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fraction → decimal | **Divide** the top by the bottom | 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = **0.375** |
| Decimal → percent | **Multiply by 100** (move the decimal point **2 places right**) and add % | 0.375 → **37.5%** |
| Percent → decimal | **Divide by 100** (move the decimal point **2 places left**) | 6% → **0.06** |
| Decimal → fraction | Write over 10, 100, 1,000… by place value, then simplify | 0.45 = 45/100 = **9/20** |
| Percent → fraction | Write over 100, then simplify | 35% = 35/100 = **7/20** |
| Fraction → percent | Divide, then multiply by 100 | 3/5 = 0.6 = **60%** |

**Memory hook:** "percent" means "per hundred." 45% = 45 per 100 = 45/100.

**Careful with small percents:** 0.5% = 0.005, not 0.5. A 7.5% sales tax is 0.075.

> **Check 1:** Write 0.08 as a percent and as a fraction in simplest form.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Mixed numbers and improper fractions

- **Mixed → improper:** multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, keep the denominator. 2 3/4 = (2 × 4 + 3)/4 = **11/4**.
- **Improper → mixed:** divide. 17/5 = 3 remainder 2 = **3 2/5**.
- **Mixed → decimal:** convert just the fraction part. 2 3/4 = 2 + 0.75 = **2.75**.

---

### 3. Simplifying fractions

Divide the top and bottom by their **greatest common factor** (the largest number that divides evenly into both). 18/24: both divide by 6 → **3/4**.

---

### 4. Benchmarks worth memorizing

| Fraction | Decimal | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/3 | 0.333… | 33.3% |
| 2/3 | 0.666… | 66.7% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% |

> **Check 2:** Write 2 3/8 as a decimal.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Moving the decimal point the wrong way.** Decimal → percent makes the number **bigger** (0.25 → 25%).
- **Forgetting the zero in small percents.** 4% = 0.04, not 0.4.
- **Dividing the wrong way.** 3/8 is 3 ÷ 8, not 8 ÷ 3.
- **Not simplifying.** The TEAS answer choices are usually in simplest form.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Fractions:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 4.1 Visualize Fractions: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-1-visualize-fractions
- **Decimals and fractions:** Section 5.3 Decimals and Fractions: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-3-decimals-and-fractions
- **Percents:** Section 6.1 Understand Percent: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/6-1-understand-percent

---

### Check answers

1. **8%** and **2/25.** 0.08 × 100 = 8%; 0.08 = 8/100 = 2/25.
2. **2.375.** 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375, plus the whole number 2.

## Arithmetic with Fractions, Decimals and Negative Numbers

**TEAS objective M.1.2** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can add, subtract, multiply and divide fractions, decimals and signed numbers, and use the order of operations.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. It may not follow the order of operations and shows fractions as decimals, so do PEMDAS one step at a time and convert back to a fraction when the answer choices are fractions.

---

### 1. Order of operations: PEMDAS

1. **P**arentheses (and other grouping symbols)
2. **E**xponents
3. **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision, **left to right**
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction, **left to right**

(An **exponent** means repeated multiplication: 4² = 4 × 4 = 16.)

**Memory hook:** Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally. Multiplication does **not** always come before division; whichever comes first, left to right, goes first.

**Example:** 20 − 3 × (4 − 2)² = 20 − 3 × 2² = 20 − 3 × 4 = 20 − 12 = **8**.

---

### 2. Fractions

| Operation | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add or subtract | Get a **common denominator** (rewrite both fractions with the same bottom number), then add or subtract the tops | 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = **5/6** |
| Multiply | Top × top, bottom × bottom | 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = **1/2** |
| Divide | **Keep, change, flip**: multiply by the **reciprocal** (the second fraction flipped upside down) | 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = **1 1/2** |

With mixed numbers, convert to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing: 1 1/2 × 2 2/3 = 3/2 × 8/3 = 24/6 = **4**.

> **Check 1:** What is 2 1/4 ÷ 3/4?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 3. Decimals

- **Add or subtract:** line up the decimal points. 12.5 + 3.75 = **16.25**.
- **Multiply:** multiply as whole numbers, then count the total decimal places in both factors. 0.3 × 0.02: 3 × 2 = 6, with 3 decimal places → **0.006**.
- **Divide:** move the decimal point in the divisor to make it whole, and move the dividend's point the same number of places. 4.5 ÷ 0.15 = 450 ÷ 15 = **30**.

---

### 4. Negative numbers

**Adding:** same signs → add and keep the sign (−4 + −6 = **−10**). Different signs → subtract the smaller **absolute value** (distance from zero, ignoring the sign) from the larger, and keep the sign of the number that is farther from zero (−9 + 4 = **−5**).

**Subtracting:** add the opposite. 3 − (−5) = 3 + 5 = **8**.

**Multiplying and dividing:**
- Same signs → **positive** (−6 × −3 = 18)
- Different signs → **negative** (−20 ÷ 4 = −5)

> **Check 2:** What is −12 ÷ (−3) − 7?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Adding denominators.** 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5.
- **Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing.** Flip the second one (the divisor).
- **Doing multiplication before an earlier division.** Go left to right.
- **Losing track of signs.** Two negatives multiply to a positive; subtracting a negative means adding.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Multiplying and dividing fractions:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 4.2 Multiply and Divide Fractions: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-2-multiply-and-divide-fractions
- **Adding and subtracting fractions:** Section 4.5 Add and Subtract Fractions with Different Denominators: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-5-add-and-subtract-fractions-with-different-denominators
- **Decimal operations:** Section 5.2 Decimal Operations: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-2-decimal-operations
- **Negative numbers:** Section 3.2 Add Integers: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/3-2-add-integers and Section 3.4 Multiply and Divide Integers: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/3-4-multiply-and-divide-integers

---

### Check answers

1. **3.** 2 1/4 = 9/4, and 9/4 × 4/3 = 36/12 = 3.
2. **−3.** −12 ÷ (−3) = 4, then 4 − 7 = −3.

## Comparing and Ordering Numbers

**TEAS objective M.1.3** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can put a mix of fractions, decimals, percents and negative numbers in order from least to greatest (or the reverse).

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it to turn each fraction into a decimal.

---

### 1. The reliable method: convert everything to decimals

1. Change every number to a decimal (fractions: divide; percents: move the point 2 left).
2. Line up the decimal points and add zeros so they have the same number of places.
3. Compare digit by digit from the left.
4. Write the answer using the **original** forms.

**Example:** order 2/3, 0.6, 65%, 0.66
- 2/3 ≈ 0.667 → 0.667
- 0.6 → 0.600
- 65% → 0.650
- 0.66 → 0.660
- Least to greatest: **0.6, 65%, 0.66, 2/3**

> **Check 1:** Order from least to greatest: 3/4, 0.7, 72%, 0.745.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Comparing fractions without decimals

- **Same denominator:** bigger numerator is bigger (5/8 > 3/8).
- **Same numerator:** **smaller** denominator is bigger (1/3 > 1/4, because the pieces are bigger).
- **Cross-multiply:** to compare 5/7 and 7/10, multiply each numerator by the *other* fraction's denominator. 5/7 gets 5 × 10 = 50; 7/10 gets 7 × 7 = 49. 50 > 49, so **5/7 > 7/10**.

---

### 3. Negative numbers

On a number line, numbers **increase to the right**. So:
- Any negative number is less than 0 and less than any positive number.
- Among negatives, the one **farther from zero is smaller**: −8 < −3.
- −0.5 > −0.75 (−0.5 is closer to zero).

**Symbols:** < means "is less than"; > means "is greater than." The symbol opens toward the bigger number.

> **Check 2:** Which is greater: −2/3 or −0.6?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Comparing the number of digits.** 0.45 > 0.405 even though 0.405 has more digits. Add zeros: 0.450 vs 0.405.
- **Thinking a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction.** 1/8 < 1/5.
- **Ordering negatives like positives.** −10 is less than −2.
- **Answering in converted form.** Give the answer in the forms the question used.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Ordering decimals:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 5.1 Decimals: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-1-decimals
- **Negative numbers on the number line:** Section 3.1 Introduction to Integers: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/3-1-introduction-to-integers
- **Comparing fractions and decimals:** Section 5.3 Decimals and Fractions: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-3-decimals-and-fractions

---

### Check answers

1. **0.7, 72%, 0.745, 3/4.** As decimals: 0.700, 0.720, 0.745, 0.750.
2. **−0.6.** −2/3 ≈ −0.667, which is farther from zero, so it is smaller.

## Solving Equations with One Variable

**TEAS objective M.1.4** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can solve linear equations in one variable, including ones with parentheses, fractions or the variable on both sides.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it for the arithmetic, but write each algebra step yourself.

---

### 1. The one rule

**Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.** Use the **opposite operation** to undo each step, working backward from the order of operations:

1. Distribute to clear **parentheses**.
2. **Combine like terms** on each side (terms with the same variable, such as 3x and 2x, or plain numbers).
3. Move the variable terms to one side (add or subtract).
4. Move the constant terms to the other side (add or subtract).
5. **Divide** (or multiply) to get the variable alone.
6. **Check** by substituting your answer into the original equation.

---

### 2. Worked examples

**Two steps:** 4x + 9 = 37
- Subtract 9: 4x = 28
- Divide by 4: **x = 7**
- Check: 4(7) + 9 = 37 ✓

**Parentheses:** 3(x − 5) = 18
- Distribute: 3x − 15 = 18
- Add 15: 3x = 33
- Divide by 3: **x = 11**

**Variable on both sides:** 7x − 4 = 3x + 20
- Subtract 3x: 4x − 4 = 20
- Add 4: 4x = 24
- Divide by 4: **x = 6**

**Fraction:** x/5 + 2 = 9
- Subtract 2: x/5 = 7
- Multiply by 5: **x = 35**

> **Check 1:** Solve 2(x + 4) = 5x − 7.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 3. Solving a formula for one letter

Treat the other letters like numbers. Solve d = rt for t: divide both sides by r → **t = d/r**.

> **Check 2:** The formula for converting temperature is F = 1.8C + 32. Find C when F = 104.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Distributing to only the first term.** 3(x − 5) is 3x − 15, not 3x − 5.
- **Sign errors when moving terms.** Subtracting a negative means adding.
- **Dividing only part of a side.** In 4x + 8 = 20, subtract 8 first; don't divide just the 4x.
- **Skipping the check.** It catches most errors in seconds.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Two-step equations:** OpenStax *Elementary Algebra 2e*, Section 2.2 Solve Equations Using the Division and Multiplication Properties of Equality: https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/2-2-solve-equations-using-the-division-and-multiplication-properties-of-equality
- **Variables on both sides:** Section 2.3 Solve Equations with Variables and Constants on Both Sides: https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/2-3-solve-equations-with-variables-and-constants-on-both-sides

---

### Check answers

1. **x = 5.** Distribute: 2x + 8 = 5x − 7. Subtract 2x: 8 = 3x − 7. Add 7: 15 = 3x. Divide by 3: x = 5. Check: 2(9) = 18 and 5(5) − 7 = 18 ✓
2. **C = 40.** 104 − 32 = 72, and 72 ÷ 1.8 = 40.

## Multi-Step Word Problems

**TEAS objective M.1.5** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can solve real-world problems that take one or several arithmetic steps: totals, differences, unit prices, rates and leftovers.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it for every multi-digit step, and estimate first so you notice a slip.

---

### 1. A four-step plan

1. **Read** the whole problem and underline **what is asked**, with its units.
2. **List** the numbers you're given, with their units. Cross out information you don't need.
3. **Plan** the steps: what must you know first to get the answer?
4. **Solve and check:** does the answer make sense? Is it in the right units?

---

### 2. Key words (clues, not rules)

| Operation | Common clue words |
|---|---|
| Add | total, sum, altogether, combined, increased by |
| Subtract | difference, how many more, left, remaining, decreased by |
| Multiply | each, per (when finding a total), times, of (with fractions/percents) |
| Divide | per (when finding a rate), split, shared equally, average, each (when finding one share) |

Clue words can mislead. Always ask what the situation actually requires.

---

### 3. Worked examples

**Total and remainder:** A nurse has a 1,000 mL bag. She infuses 125 mL per hour for 6 hours. How much is left?
- Used: 125 × 6 = 750 mL
- Left: 1,000 − 750 = **250 mL**

**Unit price (best buy):** 12 cans for $9.00, or 18 cans for $12.60. Which is cheaper per can?
- $9.00 ÷ 12 = $0.75 per can; $12.60 ÷ 18 = $0.70 per can → **the 18-pack**

**Rounding up in context:** 74 students go on a trip; each van holds 12. How many vans?
- 74 ÷ 12 = 6.17 → **7 vans** (you can't take part of a van, and 6 vans leave 2 students behind)

> **Check 1:** A student earns $14.50 an hour and works 22 hours a week. Her weekly expenses are $260. How much does she have left each week?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 4. Time and money

- Minutes to hours: divide by 60 (90 minutes = 1.5 hours).
- Hours to minutes: multiply by 60 (0.25 hour = 15 minutes).
- **24-hour clock** (used in health care): times run from 0000 (midnight) to 2359. For p.m. hours, **add 12**: 2:00 p.m. = 1400; 8:30 p.m. = 2030. After 2359 the clock restarts at 0000. Elapsed time = later time − earlier time (0800 to 1100 = 3 hours).
- Elapsed time: count up to the next whole hour, then to the end time. 8:45 to 11:20 = 15 min + 2 h + 20 min = **2 h 35 min**.

> **Check 2:** A medication is given every 6 hours, starting at 0800 (8:00 a.m.). What are the next three times on a 24-hour clock?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Answering a middle step.** If the question asks what's left, the amount used is not the answer.
- **Ignoring units.** Convert minutes to hours (or the reverse) before multiplying by a rate.
- **Rounding down when you need to round up** (vans, boxes, doses to cover a full course).
- **Using every number.** Some numbers are distractors.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Problem-solving strategy:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 9.1 Use a Problem Solving Strategy: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/9-1-use-a-problem-solving-strategy
- **Decimal operations with money:** Section 5.2 Decimal Operations: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-2-decimal-operations

---

### Check answers

1. **$59.** 14.50 × 22 = $319; $319 − $260 = $59.
2. **1400, 2000 and 0200.** Add 6 hours each time: 8:00 a.m. → 2:00 p.m. → 8:00 p.m. → 2:00 a.m.

## Percent Problems

**TEAS objective M.1.6** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can find a percent of a number, find what percent one number is of another, find the whole from a part, and work percent increase, decrease, discount, tax and tip.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Enter percents as decimals (15% → 0.15).

---

### 1. One equation covers the basics

**Part = Percent × Whole** (with the percent written as a decimal)

| Question | Set-up | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| What is 15% of 80? | Part = 0.15 × 80 | **12** |
| 12 is what percent of 80? | 12 = P × 80 → P = 12 ÷ 80 = 0.15 | **15%** |
| 12 is 15% of what number? | 12 = 0.15 × W → W = 12 ÷ 0.15 | **80** |

**Memory hook:** "of" means multiply; "is" means equals.

**Proportion method (same thing):** part/whole = percent/100. For "12 is what percent of 80?": 12/80 = x/100 → x = 15.

> **Check 1:** 18 of the 24 students in a class passed. What percent passed?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Percent change

**Percent change = (new − original) ÷ original × 100**

- Always divide by the **original** (starting) value.
- Positive = increase; negative = decrease.

**Example:** a patient's weight goes from 150 lb to 165 lb. Change = 15; 15 ÷ 150 = 0.10 → **10% increase**.

---

### 3. Discounts, tax, tip and markup

| Situation | Shortcut | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Discount of 20% | Pay 80%: × 0.80 | $45 × 0.80 = **$36** |
| Sales tax of 6% | Pay 106%: × 1.06 | $50 × 1.06 = **$53** |
| Tip of 18% | Add 18%: × 1.18 | $40 × 1.18 = **$47.20** |
| Raise of 4% | New = old × 1.04 | $20/hr × 1.04 = **$20.80/hr** |

**Two discounts in a row are not added.** 20% off, then another 10% off: $100 × 0.80 = $80, then $80 × 0.90 = **$72**, not $70.

> **Check 2:** A $250 scrub set is marked 30% off, and then 5% sales tax is added. What is the final price?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 4. Working backward to the original price

If a price is **after** a 20% discount, you paid 80% of the original. **Divide** by 0.80 to undo it.

**Example:** a jacket costs $36 after 20% off. Original = 36 ÷ 0.80 = **$45**. (Not $36 × 1.20 = $43.20: the 20% was taken from the bigger, original price.)

---

### 5. Mental-math tricks

- **10%:** move the decimal point one place left (10% of 340 = 34).
- **5%:** half of 10% (17).
- **1%:** move it two places left (3.4).
- **Build up:** 15% = 10% + 5% = 34 + 17 = **51**.

---

### Common traps

- **Dividing by the new value in percent change.** Use the original.
- **Adding stacked discounts.** Apply them one after the other.
- **Using 15 instead of 0.15.** Convert the percent to a decimal first.
- **Stopping at the discount.** If the question asks for the sale price, subtract the discount.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Percent equations:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 6.2 Solve General Applications of Percent: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/6-2-solve-general-applications-of-percent
- **Tax, discount and markup:** Section 6.3 Solve Sales Tax, Commission, and Discount Applications: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/6-3-solve-sales-tax-commission-and-discount-applications

---

### Check answers

1. **75%.** 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75.
2. **$183.75.** $250 × 0.70 = $175; $175 × 1.05 = $183.75.

## Estimation and Rounding

**TEAS objective M.1.7** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can round to a given place value, estimate answers to real-world problems, and decide when to round up or down in context.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Estimating first is the best check on a calculator answer.

---

### 1. Rounding rules

1. Find the place you are rounding to.
2. Look at the digit **just to its right**.
3. **5 or more → round up.** 4 or less → leave it.
4. Drop (or zero out) everything to the right.

| Number | Round to | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 4,672 | Nearest hundred | **4,700** |
| 3.4762 | Nearest hundredth | **3.48** |
| 12.35 | Nearest tenth | **12.4** |
| 0.0849 | Nearest hundredth | **0.08** |

**Place names:** in 3.4762, the 4 is tenths, 7 hundredths, 6 thousandths, 2 ten-thousandths.

> **Check 1:** Round 18.947 to the nearest tenth.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Estimating

Round each number to something easy, then compute.
- 49.8 × 6.1 ≈ 50 × 6 = **300** (exact: 303.78)
- 812 ÷ 39 ≈ 800 ÷ 40 = **20** (exact: about 20.8)
- $4.89 + $12.15 + $7.98 ≈ $5 + $12 + $8 = **$25** (exact: $25.02)

**Use estimates to check calculator answers.** If 49.8 × 6.1 comes out 3,037.8 on your calculator, the estimate (about 300) tells you the decimal point slipped.

---

### 3. Rounding in context

The situation decides the direction, not just the rule:
- **Round up** when you need **enough**: buses for students, boxes for items, paint cans for a wall. 74 students ÷ 12 per van = 6.17 → **7 vans**.
- **Round down** when you can only use **complete** units: how many $3 items can you buy with $20? 20 ÷ 3 = 6.67 → **6 items**.
- **Medication volumes** are rounded to what the measuring device can show: a syringe marked in tenths of a mL means rounding to the nearest tenth.

> **Check 2:** Tiles come in boxes of 12. A floor needs 130 tiles. How many boxes must you buy?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Looking at the wrong digit.** Look at the digit just right of the place you're rounding to.
- **Rounding step by step.** 3.446 to the nearest tenth is 3.4; don't round to 3.45 first and then to 3.5.
- **Always following the 5-up rule in context.** "How many vans?" always rounds up.
- **Confusing tenths and tens.** Tenths are after the decimal point.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Rounding whole numbers:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 1.1 Introduction to Whole Numbers: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/1-1-introduction-to-whole-numbers
- **Rounding decimals:** Section 5.1 Decimals: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-1-decimals

---

### Check answers

1. **18.9.** The tenths digit is 9; the next digit, 4, is less than 5, so it stays.
2. **11 boxes.** 130 ÷ 12 = 10.83; 10 boxes give only 120 tiles.

## Proportions

**TEAS objective M.1.8** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can set up and solve a proportion for real-world problems: recipes, scale drawings, dosages and unit conversions.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it for the cross-multiplying and dividing.

---

### 1. What a proportion is

A **proportion** says two ratios are equal: a/b = c/d.

**Solve by cross-multiplying:** a × d = b × c, then divide.

**Example:** 3/24 = x/40 → 24x = 3 × 40 = 120 → x = 120 ÷ 24 = **5**.

---

### 2. Setting it up correctly

The key is to keep **matching units in matching positions**:

  (thing A) / (thing B) = (thing A) / (thing B)

**Recipe:** 3 cups of flour make 24 cookies. How much for 40 cookies?
- cups/cookies = cups/cookies → 3/24 = x/40 → **x = 5 cups**

**Map scale:** 1 inch = 25 miles. Two towns are 3.5 inches apart.
- 1/25 = 3.5/x → x = 25 × 3.5 = **87.5 miles**

> **Check 1:** A car uses 8 gallons of gas to drive 248 miles. How many gallons will it use for 403 miles?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 3. Dosage calculations

**Dose on hand / amount it comes in = dose ordered / x** (the same idea as the nursing formula: Desired ÷ Have × Quantity)

**Example:** a liquid has 250 mg in 5 mL. The order is 400 mg. How many mL?
- 250 mg / 5 mL = 400 mg / x mL
- Cross-multiply: 250 × x = 5 × 400, so 250x = 2,000
- Divide by 250: **x = 8 mL**
- **Sanity check:** the order (400 mg) is more than what's in 5 mL (250 mg), so the answer must be more than 5 mL. ✓

**Example (tablets):** tablets are 25 mg each. The order is 75 mg.
- 25 mg / 1 tablet = 75 mg / x → **x = 3 tablets**

> **Check 2:** A medication is supplied as 40 mg per 2 mL. How many mL contain 30 mg?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 4. Direct vs inverse

In a proportion, both quantities rise together (**direct**). Some problems are **inverse**: more workers → **less** time. If 4 workers take 6 hours, 8 workers take **3 hours** (4 × 6 = 8 × 3). Don't set up an inverse situation as a regular proportion.

---

### Common traps

- **Flipping one ratio.** Put the same units on top on both sides.
- **Mixing units.** Convert first (for example, grams to milligrams).
- **Forgetting the sanity check.** Ask whether the answer should be bigger or smaller than what you started with.
- **Using a proportion for inverse situations.** More workers means less time.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Proportions and their applications:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 6.5 Solve Proportions and their Applications: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/6-5-solve-proportions-and-their-applications

---

2. **1.5 mL.** 40/2 = 30/x → 40x = 60 → x = 1.5.

## Ratios, Rates and Rates of Change

**TEAS objective M.1.9** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can write and simplify ratios, find unit rates, use distance-rate-time, and describe a rate of change.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it for unit rates; convert minutes to hours first.

---

### 1. Ratios

A **ratio** compares two quantities: 12 to 18, 12:18 or 12/18.
- **Order matters.** "Men to women" puts men first.
- **Simplify** like a fraction: 12:18 → divide both by 6 → **2:3**.
- **Part-to-part vs part-to-whole:** with 12 men and 18 women, men to women is 2:3, but men to the **whole class** (30) is 12:30 = **2:5**.

**Splitting by a ratio:** share $600 in the ratio 2:3. Total parts = 5; one part = $120. Shares: **$240 and $360**.

> **Check 1:** A solution mixes concentrate and water in a 1:4 ratio. How much concentrate is in 500 mL of the solution?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Rates and unit rates

A **rate** compares quantities with **different units**: miles per hour, dollars per pound, drops per minute.

A **unit rate** has 1 in the denominator. Find it by dividing.
- 150 miles in 2.5 hours → 150 ÷ 2.5 = **60 miles per hour**
- $3.60 for 12 oz → 3.60 ÷ 12 = **$0.30 per ounce**

---

### 3. Distance = rate × time

**d = r × t**, so r = d ÷ t and t = d ÷ r.

**Example:** a trip of 195 miles at 65 mph takes 195 ÷ 65 = **3 hours**.

**Watch the units:** at 60 mph for 45 minutes, convert 45 minutes to 0.75 hour → 60 × 0.75 = **45 miles**.

---

### 4. Rate of change

**Rate of change = change in output ÷ change in input** (the slope of a line).

**Example:** a child weighed 18 kg at age 4 and 24 kg at age 7. Rate of change = (24 − 18) ÷ (7 − 4) = 6 ÷ 3 = **2 kg per year**.

A positive rate means increasing; a negative rate means decreasing.

> **Check 2:** An IV bag held 1,000 mL at 0800 and 640 mL at 1100. What was the average rate of change per hour?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Reversing the order of a ratio.** Write it in the order the question names.
- **Confusing part-to-part with part-to-whole.**
- **Forgetting to convert minutes to hours** in rate problems.
- **Dropping the sign of a rate of change.** A decrease is negative.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Ratios and rates:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 5.6 Ratios and Rate: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-6-ratios-and-rate
- **Slope as rate of change:** OpenStax *Elementary Algebra 2e*, Section 4.4 Understand Slope of a Line: https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/4-4-understand-slope-of-a-line

---

### Check answers

1. **100 mL.** 1 + 4 = 5 parts; 500 ÷ 5 = 100 mL per part, and concentrate is 1 part.
2. **−120 mL per hour.** (640 − 1,000) ÷ 3 hours = −360 ÷ 3 = −120. The bag is draining at 120 mL per hour.

## Expressions, Equations and Inequalities in Real Life

**TEAS objective M.1.10** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can translate a real situation into an expression, equation or inequality, evaluate it, and solve simple inequalities.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it after you have set up the equation.

---

### 1. Translating words into algebra

| Words | Algebra |
|---|---|
| 5 more than a number | x + 5 |
| 5 less than a number | **x − 5** (order flips!) |
| twice a number | 2x |
| a number divided by 4 | x/4 |
| 3 times the sum of a number and 2 | 3(x + 2) |
| is, equals, will be | = |
| at least, no less than, minimum | **≥** |
| at most, no more than, maximum | **≤** |
| is more than, is greater than, exceeds | > |
| is less than, is fewer than | < |

**Watch for "is."** "5 less than x" is an expression (x − 5). "5 is less than x" is an inequality (5 < x).

**Fixed + variable cost model:** total = (one-time amount) + (rate × number of units).
- A gym charges $50 to join plus $25 a month: **C = 50 + 25m**.
- A taxi charges $3 plus $2.50 per mile: **C = 3 + 2.5d**.

> **Check 1:** A phone plan costs $30 a month plus $0.10 per text. Write an expression for one month's cost with t texts, then evaluate it for 150 texts.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Evaluating expressions

Substitute, then follow the order of operations.
- For 3x² − 2x with x = 4: 3(16) − 8 = 48 − 8 = **40**.
- Use parentheses when substituting negatives: x = −2 in x² gives (−2)² = **4**.

---

### 3. Solving inequalities

**Story:** Maria needs at least $600 for tuition. She has $150 saved and earns $15 an hour. How many hours, h, must she work?

Solve like an equation, **except: when you multiply or divide by a negative number, flip the inequality sign.**

- 150 + 15h ≥ 600 → 15h ≥ 450 → **h ≥ 30**
- −3x < 12 → divide by −3 and flip → **x > −4**

**Reading the answer in context:** h ≥ 30 means she must work **at least 30 hours**.

> **Check 2:** Solve: 7 − 2x ≤ 1.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 4. Writing an equation from a story

**Example:** a rental costs $40 plus $0.25 per mile. The bill was $65. How many miles?
- 40 + 0.25m = 65 → 0.25m = 25 → **m = 100 miles**

---

### Common traps

- **Reversing "less than."** "5 less than x" is x − 5, not 5 − x.
- **Choosing ≤ for "at least."** At least means ≥.
- **Forgetting to flip the sign** when dividing by a negative.
- **Charging a one-time fee every month.** The fixed part isn't multiplied by the variable.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Translating and evaluating expressions:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 2.2 Evaluate, Simplify, and Translate Expressions: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/2-2-evaluate-simplify-and-translate-expressions
- **Inequalities:** OpenStax *Elementary Algebra 2e*, Section 2.7 Solve Linear Inequalities: https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/2-7-solve-linear-inequalities
- **Word problems:** Section 3.1 Use a Problem-Solving Strategy: https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/3-1-use-a-problem-solving-strategy

1. **30 + 0.10t; $45.** 30 + 0.10 × 150 = 30 + 15 = $45.
2. **x ≥ 3.** Subtract 7: −2x ≤ −6. Divide by −2 and flip: x ≥ 3.

### 1. Read before you calculate

1. **Title:** what is being shown?
2. **Axis labels and units:** what does each axis measure, and in what units?
3. **Scale:** how much is each grid line worth (1, 5, 20…)?
4. **Key or legend:** what do colors or symbols mean?

### 2. Common graph types

| Graph | Best for | Read it by |
|---|---|---|
| **Table** | Exact values | Find the row and column |
| **Bar graph** | Comparing categories | Bar height against the axis |
| **Line graph** | Change over time | Slope: steep = fast change; flat = no change |
| **Pie chart** | Parts of a whole | Each slice is a percent of 100% |
| **Pictograph** | Counts with symbols | Check the key: one symbol may equal 10 items |

- Largest increase between consecutive months: March to April, **+40**.

**Using the line graph:**
- Temperature at 3 hours: **100.2 °F**.
- Total drop over 6 hours: 101.8 − 98.8 = **3.0 °F**.
- The line flattens between hours 5 and 6, so the drop slowed down.

> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 3. Pie charts

Slices are percents that add to 100%. To find an amount: **percent × total**.

**Example:** a $2,400 monthly budget is shown as rent 40%, food 15%, car 20%, other 25%. Rent = 0.40 × 2,400 = **$960**.

> **Check 2:** In that budget, how much more goes to the car than to food?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

- **Misreading the scale.** If grid lines go by 20, a bar halfway between 140 and 160 is 150.
- **Using the wrong row or column** in a table. Run your finger along both.
- **Confusing "change" with "value."** The question may ask how much something increased, not its final value.
- **Bringing in outside knowledge.** Answer only from the data shown.

## Mean, Median, Mode and Range

**TEAS objective M.2.2** · Measurement and Data

**What the TEAS expects:** you can calculate the mean, median, mode and range of a data set, find a missing value from a given mean, and judge which measure best describes data with outliers.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it to add long lists of values.

---

### 1. The four measures

Data set: **3, 5, 5, 8, 9**

| Measure | How to find it | Here |
|---|---|---|
| **Mean** (average) | Add all values, divide by how many | 30 ÷ 5 = **6** |
| **Median** | Put in order; take the **middle** value | **5** |
| **Mode** | The value that appears **most often** | **5** |
| **Range** | Largest − smallest | 9 − 3 = **6** |

**Even number of values:** the median is the **average of the two middle values**. For 2, 4, 7, 10 → (4 + 7) ÷ 2 = **5.5**.

A data set can have **no mode** (all values different) or **more than one** mode.

**Memory hook:** **MO**de = **MO**st; **MED**ian = **MID**dle (like the median of a highway).

> **Check 1:** Find the mean, median, mode and range of: 12, 15, 11, 15, 22, 9.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Finding a missing value

**Total = mean × number of values.**

**Example:** a student's first four test scores are 82, 75, 90 and 88. What score on the fifth test gives an average of 85?
- Needed total: 85 × 5 = 425
- Current total: 82 + 75 + 90 + 88 = 335
- Needed score: 425 − 335 = **90**

---

### 3. Mean from a frequency table

| Score | Number of students |
|---|---|
| 70 | 2 |
| 80 | 5 |
| 90 | 3 |

Multiply each value by its count and add: 70 × 2 + 80 × 5 + 90 × 3 = 140 + 400 + 270 = 810. Divide by the total count (2 + 5 + 3 = 10): mean = **81**.

---

### 4. Outliers

An **outlier** is a value far from the rest. It pulls the **mean** toward it but barely moves the **median**.

**Example:** salaries $40k, $42k, $45k, $47k, $300k.
- Mean = $474k ÷ 5 = **$94.8k** (higher than four of the five salaries)
- Median = **$45k** (a better picture of the typical salary)

**Rule of thumb:** with outliers or skewed data, the **median** is the better "typical" value.

> **Check 2:** Five patients wait 10, 12, 12, 14 and 52 minutes. Which better describes a typical wait, the mean or the median? Give both.
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Finding the median without ordering the data first.**
- **Forgetting to average the two middle values** when there is an even count.
- **Confusing range with the largest value.** Range is a difference.
- **Using the mean for skewed data.** An outlier distorts it.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Mean, median and mode:** OpenStax *Introductory Statistics 2e*, Section 2.5 Measures of the Center of the Data: https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/2-5-measures-of-the-center-of-the-data
- **Range and spread:** Section 2.7 Measures of the Spread of the Data: https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/2-7-measures-of-the-spread-of-the-data
- **Averages, with simpler examples:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 5.5 Averages and Probability: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-5-averages-and-probability

---

2. **The median (12 minutes).** The mean is 100 ÷ 5 = 20 minutes, pulled up by the 52-minute outlier.

## Relationships Between Two Variables

**TEAS objective M.2.3** · Measurement and Data

**What the TEAS expects:** you can identify the independent and dependent variables, describe a relationship as positive, negative or none, read a scatter plot, and interpret the slope of a line.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it to find a slope from two points.

---

### 1. Independent and dependent variables

- The **independent variable** is the input: the thing that's chosen or changed on purpose. It goes on the **x-axis** (horizontal).
- The **dependent variable** is the output, the result that **depends on** the independent variable. It goes on the **y-axis** (vertical).

**Test:** say "___ depends on ___." Test score depends on hours studied, so hours studied is independent.

> **Check 1:** A study tracks how a person's heart rate changes with the speed of the treadmill. Which is the dependent variable?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### 2. Scatter plots and correlation

| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Positive correlation** | Points rise from left to right: as x increases, y tends to increase |
| **Negative correlation** | Points fall from left to right: as x increases, y tends to decrease |
| **No correlation** | No clear pattern |

The **closer the points are to a straight line, the stronger** the relationship.

**Correlation is not causation.** Two variables can move together because a third factor drives both (S.4.2 covers this).

---

### 3. Lines and slope

A straight line can be written **y = mx + b**:
- **m (slope)** = rate of change = rise ÷ run = how much y changes when x increases by 1.
- **b (y-intercept)** = the value of y when x = 0 (the starting value).

**Example:** a plumber charges y = 75x + 50, where x is hours. The **$50** is the call-out fee (charged even at 0 hours); the **$75** is the hourly rate. Each extra hour adds $75.

**Slope from two points:** (2, 9) and (6, 21) → m = (21 − 9) ÷ (6 − 2) = 12 ÷ 4 = **3**.

> **Check 2:** In y = −2x + 10, what happens to y when x increases by 3?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Swapping the axes.** Independent on x, dependent on y.
- **Reading a negative slope as "no relationship."** Falling points show a negative relationship; how tightly they hug a line shows how strong it is.
- **Treating the intercept as a rate.** The intercept is a one-time starting value.
- **Concluding causation from a scatter plot.**

---

### Learn it properly

- **Scatter plots:** OpenStax *Introductory Statistics 2e*, Section 12.2 Scatter Plots: https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/12-2-scatter-plots
- **Linear equations, slope and intercept:** Section 12.1 Linear Equations: https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/12-1-linear-equations
- **Slope:** OpenStax *Elementary Algebra 2e*, Section 4.4 Understand Slope of a Line: https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/4-4-understand-slope-of-a-line

---

### Check answers

1. **Heart rate.** It depends on the treadmill speed, which the study sets.
2. **y decreases by 6.** The slope is −2, so each 1-unit increase in x lowers y by 2; 3 × (−2) = −6.

## Geometry: Perimeter, Area, Volume and Angles

**TEAS objective M.2.4** · Measurement and Data

**What the TEAS expects:** you can calculate perimeter, area, circumference and volume of common shapes, use the Pythagorean theorem, and use angle facts. The TEAS usually gives π or asks you to use 3.14.

**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use 3.14 for π unless the question says otherwise.

---

### 1. Perimeter and area

| Shape | Perimeter (distance around) | Area (space inside) |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectangle** (length l, width w) | 2l + 2w | **l × w** |
| **Square** (side s) | 4s | **s²** |
| **Triangle** (base b, height h) | add the three sides | **½ × b × h** |
| **Circle** (radius r; diameter d = 2r) | Circumference = **2πr** = πd | **πr²** |
| **Parallelogram** | add the sides | b × h |
| **Trapezoid** (parallel sides a and b) | add the sides | ½ × (a + b) × h |

**Units:** perimeter is in plain units (ft); area is in **square** units (ft²).

**Example:** a circle with diameter 10 cm has r = 5. Circumference = 2 × 3.14 × 5 = **31.4 cm**. Area = 3.14 × 5² = **78.5 cm²**.

> **Check 1:** A room is 12 ft by 14 ft. Carpet costs $3.50 per square foot. How much will carpeting the room cost?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

**Composite shapes:** split into rectangles (or add and subtract pieces). An L-shaped room made of a 10 × 12 ft rectangle and a 6 × 4 ft rectangle has area 120 + 24 = **144 ft²**.

**Missing side:** if a rectangle has area 96 ft² and length 12 ft, its width is 96 ÷ 12 = **8 ft**.

---

### 2. Volume

| Solid | Volume |
|---|---|
| **Rectangular prism** (box) | **l × w × h** |
| **Cube** | **s³** |
| **Cylinder** | **πr²h** |

Volume is in **cubic** units (cm³). Remember 1 cm³ = 1 mL.

**Example:** a tank 50 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm holds 30,000 cm³ = **30,000 mL = 30 L**.

---

### 3. Right triangles: the Pythagorean theorem

In a right triangle with **legs** a and b (the two sides that form the right angle) and **hypotenuse** c (the longest side, opposite the right angle):

**a² + b² = c²**

**Example:** a 10-ft ladder leans against a wall with its base 6 ft from the wall. How high does it reach? 6² + b² = 10² → 36 + b² = 100 → b² = 64 → **b = 8 ft**.

**Undoing a square:** take the **square root**. √64 = 8 because 8 × 8 = 64. Learn the perfect squares from 1² = 1 to 15² = 225; if the calculator has no √ key, use them to find the answer.

Common whole-number triples: **3-4-5**, **5-12-13**, **6-8-10**.

---

### 4. Angle facts

- A **straight line** = 180°. A **full turn** = 360°.
- The angles of a **triangle** add to **180°**. A **quadrilateral**'s add to **360°**.
- **Right angle** = 90°; **acute** < 90°; **obtuse** between 90° and 180°.
- **Complementary** angles add to 90°; **supplementary** angles add to 180°.

> **Check 2:** Two angles of a triangle are 47° and 68°. What is the third angle?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

### Common traps

- **Using the diameter in a radius formula.** Halve it first.
- **Forgetting ½ in the triangle area.**
- **Mixing perimeter and area.** Fencing is perimeter; carpet is area.
- **Wrong units.** Area is square units; volume is cubic units.

---

### Learn it properly

- **Rectangles, triangles and trapezoids:** OpenStax *Prealgebra 2e*, Section 9.4 Use Properties of Rectangles, Triangles, and Trapezoids: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/9-4-use-properties-of-rectangles-triangles-and-trapezoids
- **Circles:** Section 9.5 Solve Geometry Applications: Circles and Irregular Figures: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/9-5-solve-geometry-applications-circles-and-irregular-figures
- **Volume:** Section 9.6 Solve Geometry Applications: Volume and Surface Area: https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/9-6-solve-geometry-applications-volume-and-surface-area

### 4. The factor-label (dimensional analysis) method

Multiply by fractions equal to 1, arranged so the units you don't want **cancel**.

**Example:** convert 165 lb to kg.
165 lb × (1 kg / 2.2 lb) = **75 kg**. (lb cancels.)

**Two steps:** convert 2 cups to mL.
2 cups × (8 fl oz / 1 cup) × (30 mL / 1 fl oz) = **480 mL**.

**Weight-based dose:** 2 mg per kg for a 154-lb patient.
154 lb ÷ 2.2 = 70 kg; 70 × 2 mg = **140 mg**.

### Common traps

- **Multiplying when you should divide.** kg is a bigger unit than lb, so the kg number is smaller.
- **Mixing fluid ounces (volume) with ounces (weight).**
- **Skipping a step** in a two-step conversion.
- **Forgetting to convert the patient's weight to kg** before a per-kg dose.
