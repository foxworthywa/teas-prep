Acids, Bases and pH

TEAS objective S.3.6 · Chemistry

What the TEAS expects: you can describe acids and bases, read the pH scale (including the tenfold rule), predict the products of neutralization, and explain what a buffer does.

1. Acids and bases

Swipe the table sideways to see every column. →

Acids Bases
Definition Release H⁺ (hydrogen ions) in water; proton donors (an H⁺ ion is just a proton) Release OH⁻ (hydroxide ions) or accept H⁺; proton acceptors
pH Below 7 Above 7
Taste and feel Sour Bitter, slippery
Litmus paper Turns red Turns blue
Examples Stomach acid (HCl), vinegar, lemon juice, carbonic acid Sodium hydroxide (lye), ammonia, baking soda, bleach

Strong vs weak: a strong acid or base ionizes completely (breaks apart into ions) in water (HCl, NaOH). A weak one ionizes only partly (acetic acid in vinegar, ammonia). "Strong" is about how completely it ionizes, not how concentrated it is.

2. The pH scale

  • pH runs from 0 to 14. 7 is neutral (pure water).
  • Lower pH = more H⁺ = more acidic.
  • Each pH unit is a tenfold change in H⁺ concentration. pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4 and 100 times more acidic than pH 5.
  • Normal blood pH is 7.35–7.45, slightly basic. Below 7.35 is acidosis; above 7.45 is alkalosis. Stomach acid is about pH 1.5–3.5.

Check 1: Solution X has pH 2 and solution Y has pH 5. How many times greater is the H⁺ concentration of X? Answer at the bottom of the page.

3. Neutralization

Acid + base → salt + water.

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

The H⁺ from the acid and the OH⁻ from the base combine to form water; the leftover ions form a salt. Antacids work this way: a weak base (such as calcium carbonate or magnesium hydroxide) neutralizes excess stomach acid.

4. Buffers

A buffer resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added. It is a mix of a weak acid and its matching base, so it can absorb extra H⁺ or extra OH⁻.

The main buffer in blood is the carbonic acid–bicarbonate system (H₂CO₃ / HCO₃⁻). CO₂ dissolved in blood forms carbonic acid, so the lungs (by exhaling CO₂, which raises pH) and kidneys (by adjusting bicarbonate) help keep blood pH in its narrow range.

Check 2: What is the main job of a buffer? Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Reading pH backwards. A lower pH is more acidic.
  • Treating pH steps as equal amounts. Each step is ×10, so two steps is ×100.
  • Confusing strong with concentrated. Strong means fully ionized.
  • Calling blood neutral. Normal blood is slightly basic (7.35–7.45).
