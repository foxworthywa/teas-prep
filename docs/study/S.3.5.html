TEAS objective S.3.5 · Chemistry

What the TEAS expects: you can use solution vocabulary, explain what affects solubility, work simple concentration and dilution problems, and predict the direction of osmosis.

1. Vocabulary

  • A solution is a homogeneous mixture: evenly mixed throughout, so every part has the same composition (salt water, air).
  • The solute is what gets dissolved (salt). The solvent does the dissolving (water). Water is the "universal solvent."
  • A heterogeneous mixture is not evenly mixed; you can often see the parts (salad, sand in water).
  • Suspension: particles are large enough to settle out over time (muddy water). Colloid: particles are small enough to stay spread out but large enough to scatter light (milk).
  • Unsaturated: can still dissolve more solute. Saturated: holding the maximum at that temperature. Supersaturated: holding more than the maximum (unstable; extra solute can suddenly crystallize).

2. What dissolves in what

"Like dissolves like": polar solvents dissolve polar and ionic solutes; nonpolar solvents dissolve nonpolar solutes. Salt and sugar dissolve in water; oil does not.

Factors that affect solubility and dissolving speed:

Factor Solids in a liquid Gases in a liquid
Higher temperature Usually more soluble Less soluble (warm soda goes flat faster)
Higher pressure Little effect More soluble (soda is bottled under pressure)

Stirring and crushing the solute make it dissolve faster but do not change how much can dissolve.

3. Concentration

  • Percent (w/v, weight/volume) = grams of solute per 100 mL of solution. A 0.9% saline solution has 0.9 g of NaCl per 100 mL.
  • Molarity (M) = moles of solute per liter of solution. (A mole is a counting unit for particles, like a dozen but far larger: 6.02 × 10²³.) 1 M NaCl has 1 mole of NaCl in each liter.
  • Dilution: adding solvent lowers concentration but not the amount of solute. Use C₁V₁ = C₂V₂.

Worked example: how much 10% solution do you need to make 200 mL of 2% solution? C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ → 10% × V₁ = 2% × 200 mL → V₁ = (2 × 200) ÷ 10 = 40 mL (then add water up to 200 mL).

4. Osmosis and tonicity

Osmosis is the movement of water across a selectively permeable (semipermeable) membrane toward the side with more solute. Memory hook: water follows salt.

Swipe the table sideways to see every column. →

Solution around a cell Compared with the cell Water moves Red blood cell
Isotonic (0.9% saline) Same solute concentration No net movement Normal
Hypotonic (pure water) Lower Into the cell Swells, may burst (hemolysis)
Hypertonic (3% saline) Higher Out of the cell Shrinks (crenates)

This is why IV fluids for most patients are isotonic, such as 0.9% normal saline.

Common traps

  • Swapping solute and solvent. The solute is the thing dissolved.
  • Thinking stirring increases solubility. It only speeds up dissolving.
  • Assuming heat helps gases dissolve. Warmer liquids hold less gas.
  • Getting osmosis backwards. Water moves toward higher solute concentration.
