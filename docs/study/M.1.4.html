TEAS objective M.1.4 · Number and Algebra

What the TEAS expects: you can solve linear equations in one variable, including ones with parentheses, fractions or the variable on both sides.

Calculator: the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it for the arithmetic, but write each algebra step yourself.

1. The one rule

Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Use the opposite operation to undo each step, working backward from the order of operations:

  1. Distribute to clear parentheses.
  2. Combine like terms on each side (terms with the same variable, such as 3x and 2x, or plain numbers).
  3. Move the variable terms to one side (add or subtract).
  4. Move the constant terms to the other side (add or subtract).
  5. Divide (or multiply) to get the variable alone.
  6. Check by substituting your answer into the original equation.

2. Worked examples

Two steps: 4x + 9 = 37

  • Subtract 9: 4x = 28
  • Divide by 4: x = 7
  • Check: 4(7) + 9 = 37 ✓

Parentheses: 3(x − 5) = 18

  • Distribute: 3x − 15 = 18
  • Add 15: 3x = 33
  • Divide by 3: x = 11

Variable on both sides: 7x − 4 = 3x + 20

  • Subtract 3x: 4x − 4 = 20
  • Add 4: 4x = 24
  • Divide by 4: x = 6

Fraction: x/5 + 2 = 9

  • Subtract 2: x/5 = 7
  • Multiply by 5: x = 35

Check 1: Solve 2(x + 4) = 5x − 7. Answer at the bottom of the page.

3. Solving a formula for one letter

Treat the other letters like numbers. Solve d = rt for t: divide both sides by r → t = d/r.

Check 2: The formula for converting temperature is F = 1.8C + 32. Find C when F = 104. Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Distributing to only the first term. 3(x − 5) is 3x − 15, not 3x − 5.
  • Sign errors when moving terms. Subtracting a negative means adding.
  • Dividing only part of a side. In 4x + 8 = 20, subtract 8 first; don't divide just the 4x.
  • Skipping the check. It catches most errors in seconds.
