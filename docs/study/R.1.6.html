TEAS objective R.1.6 · Key Ideas and Details

What the TEAS expects: you can put events or steps in order and tell what happened before, after, or immediately after something else.

1. Signal words

Signal Examples
Order first, second, next, then, finally, last
Time before, after, while, during, later, meanwhile, by the time
Dates and clock times In 1854…, At eleven o'clock…

2. Text order isn't always time order

Writers sometimes flash back or mention a later event early. Use dates and signal words, not just the order of the sentences.

In 2024 Rosa graduated from nursing school. Four years earlier, she had started college. → Starting college (2020) came first, even though the sentence about it comes second.

Check 1: Before the meeting, Sam printed the agenda. After the meeting ended, he emailed the notes. During the meeting, he took attendance. What did Sam do second? Answer at the bottom of the page.

3. "Immediately after" questions

"Immediately after" means the very next event, not just something later. Make a quick list:

  1. Resident explains the problem
  2. Aide reports it to the nurse
  3. Nurse calls the kitchen
  4. Food arrives

What happened immediately after step 1? Step 2, not step 3 or 4.

4. Ordering steps in directions

For process questions, ask what must happen first for the next step to work (you can't rinse off soap you haven't applied).

Check 2: Put these handwashing steps in order: (A) Rinse your hands. (B) Wet your hands. (C) Dry your hands. (D) Lather with soap and scrub. Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Assuming the first sentence describes the first event.
  • Treating "after" as "immediately after."
  • Skipping a step when building a sequence.
