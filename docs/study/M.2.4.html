TEAS objective M.2.4 · Measurement and Data

What the TEAS expects: you can calculate perimeter, area, circumference and volume of common shapes, use the Pythagorean theorem, and use angle facts. The TEAS usually gives π or asks you to use 3.14.

Calculator: the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use 3.14 for π unless the question says otherwise.

1. Perimeter and area

Swipe the table sideways to see every column. →

Shape Perimeter (distance around) Area (space inside)
Rectangle (length l, width w) 2l + 2w l × w
Square (side s) 4s s²
Triangle (base b, height h) add the three sides ½ × b × h
Circle (radius r; diameter d = 2r) Circumference = 2πr = πd πr²
Parallelogram add the sides b × h
Trapezoid (parallel sides a and b) add the sides ½ × (a + b) × h

Units: perimeter is in plain units (ft); area is in square units (ft²).

Example: a circle with diameter 10 cm has r = 5. Circumference = 2 × 3.14 × 5 = 31.4 cm. Area = 3.14 × 5² = 78.5 cm².

Check 1: A room is 12 ft by 14 ft. Carpet costs $3.50 per square foot. How much will carpeting the room cost? Answer at the bottom of the page.

Composite shapes: split into rectangles (or add and subtract pieces). An L-shaped room made of a 10 × 12 ft rectangle and a 6 × 4 ft rectangle has area 120 + 24 = 144 ft².

Missing side: if a rectangle has area 96 ft² and length 12 ft, its width is 96 ÷ 12 = 8 ft.

2. Volume

Solid Volume
Rectangular prism (box) l × w × h
Cube s³
Cylinder πr²h

Volume is in cubic units (cm³). Remember 1 cm³ = 1 mL.

Example: a tank 50 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm holds 30,000 cm³ = 30,000 mL = 30 L.

3. Right triangles: the Pythagorean theorem

In a right triangle with legs a and b (the two sides that form the right angle) and hypotenuse c (the longest side, opposite the right angle):

a² + b² = c²

Example: a 10-ft ladder leans against a wall with its base 6 ft from the wall. How high does it reach? 6² + b² = 10² → 36 + b² = 100 → b² = 64 → b = 8 ft.

Undoing a square: take the square root. √64 = 8 because 8 × 8 = 64. Learn the perfect squares from 1² = 1 to 15² = 225; if the calculator has no √ key, use them to find the answer.

Common whole-number triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 6-8-10.

4. Angle facts

  • A straight line = 180°. A full turn = 360°.
  • The angles of a triangle add to 180°. A quadrilateral's add to 360°.
  • Right angle = 90°; acute < 90°; obtuse between 90° and 180°.
  • Complementary angles add to 90°; supplementary angles add to 180°.

Check 2: Two angles of a triangle are 47° and 68°. What is the third angle? Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Using the diameter in a radius formula. Halve it first.
  • Forgetting ½ in the triangle area.
  • Mixing perimeter and area. Fencing is perimeter; carpet is area.
  • Wrong units. Area is square units; volume is cubic units.
