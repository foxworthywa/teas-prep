Punctuation

TEAS objective E.1.2 · Conventions of Standard English

What the TEAS expects: you can use commas, semicolons, colons, apostrophes, and quotation marks correctly.

Words you need

  • Clause: a group of words with a subject and a verb.
  • Independent clause: a clause that can stand alone as a sentence. The test was long.
  • Dependent clause: a clause that starts with a word like because, when, although, if, or since and can't stand alone. Because the test was long
  • Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS): the seven joining words for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.

1. Commas

Use a comma… Example
After an introductory word, phrase, or clause After the shift ended, the nurse wrote her notes.
Between items in a series Bring your ID, two pencils, and a water bottle.
Before a FANBOYS word (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) joining two complete sentences The test was long, but she finished on time.
Around extra information that could be removed without changing who or what you mean Dr. Lee, the new surgeon, starts Monday.
In dates and addresses May 4, 2026, in Norfolk, Virginia

The serial (Oxford) comma, before "and" in a list, is standard in academic writing, and these pages always use it. Some styles (such as newspapers) leave it out; whichever you choose, be consistent.

Don't put a comma:

  • between a subject and its verb ("The nurse, wrote" ✗);
  • between two complete sentences with no joining word (a comma splice);
  • around information you need to know which one is meant: The nurse who works nights is here (no commas: "who works nights" tells you which nurse). Clauses starting with that never take commas; clauses starting with which usually do.

Check 1: Add the needed comma: "Because the clinic was busy the patients waited." Answer at the bottom of the page.

2. Semicolons and colons

  • Semicolon (;): joins two complete sentences that are closely related. I studied every night; I felt ready. Also separates list items that already contain commas.
  • However, therefore, for example: when one of these words connects two complete sentences, put a semicolon before it and a comma after it: I studied every night; therefore, I felt ready. A comma alone before "however" makes a comma splice: "I studied, however I felt nervous" ✗.
  • Colon (:): introduces a list, explanation, or example, after a complete sentence. Bring three things: your ID, a pencil, and water. Not after "such as" or a verb: "Bring: your ID" ✗.

3. Apostrophes

Use Rule Example
Singular possessive add 's (even if the word ends in s) the patient's chart; the boss's office
Plural possessive (ends in s) add ' the patients' charts
Plural possessive (no s) add 's the children's toys
Contraction apostrophe replaces missing letters do not → don't

Never use an apostrophe to make a plain plural ("three patient's" ✗) or with possessive pronouns (its, yours, hers, theirs).

Check 2: Which is correct for the charts of several nurses: nurse's charts, nurses' charts, nurses's charts? Answer at the bottom of the page.

4. Quotation marks

In American English, periods and commas go inside closing quotation marks: She said, "I'm ready." Colons and semicolons go outside. Question marks go inside only if the quotation itself is a question.

5. Capitalization

Capitalize:

  • The first word of a sentence and of a full-sentence quotation. She said, "We start at noon."
  • Proper nouns: names of specific people, places, organizations, days, months, and holidays. Dr. Lee, Norfolk, Eastern Shore Community College, Monday, July
  • Titles before a name (Doctor Patel), but not alone (the doctor arrived).
  • Main words in titles of books and articles. Writing Guide with Handbook

Don't capitalize seasons (spring), general subjects (biology, but English and Spanish, which are languages), or directions (drive north) unless they name a region (the South).

Common traps

  • Comma splices. Two sentences need a period, a semicolon, or a comma plus a FANBOYS word.
  • "However" with only commas between two sentences. Use a semicolon before it.
  • Apostrophes in plurals. "Patients" (more than one) has no apostrophe.
  • Colons after incomplete sentences.
