TEAS objective R.1.3 · Key Ideas and Details

What the TEAS expects: you can read a set of directions, such as instructions, a label, or a procedure, and figure out exactly what a person should do in a given situation.

1. Read directions like a nurse reads an order

  1. Find the situation in the question (who, how old, what happened, what time).
  2. Find the matching line in the directions. Labels and instructions are often organized by age, dose, or step.
  3. Read the whole line, including exceptions. Words such as unless, except, only, before, after, do not, and ask a doctor change everything.
  4. Pick the choice that matches exactly, not the one that sounds most helpful.

2. Words that change the meaning

Word or phrase What it tells you
unless / except an exception to the rule
before / after / within timing
do not / never / stop a prohibition
ask a doctor / call the person shouldn't decide alone
every 24 hours how often (timing)
not more than … in 24 hours a limit

Check 1: A label says: "Adults: 2 tablets every 6 hours. Do not take more than 8 tablets in 24 hours. Children under 12: ask a doctor." What should a 10-year-old's parent do? Answer at the bottom of the page.

3. Directions with steps

When directions are numbered, the order matters. If a question describes someone who did the steps in the wrong order or skipped one, find the first step that went wrong.

Check 2: Directions: "1. Wash your hands. 2. Put on gloves. 3. Remove the old bandage. 4. Clean the wound." A student put on gloves, then washed her hands. What should she have done first? Answer at the bottom of the page.

4. Directions that change something step by step

Some TEAS questions give a starting word or number and a list of changes. Write the result after every step on your scratch paper; don't try to hold it all in your head.

Example: Start with the word HEART.

  1. Remove the last letter. → HEAR
  2. Change the first letter to B. → BEAR
  3. Add the letter D to the end. → BEARD

Check 3: Start with the number 25. Double it. Subtract 10. Divide the result by 4. What number do you end with? Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Answering from experience instead of from the directions given.
  • Missing the age or weight group. Check which line applies.
  • Ignoring "ask a doctor." On the TEAS, if the label says to ask, the answer is to ask.

Check answers

  1. Ask a doctor. The adult dose doesn't apply to children under 12.
  2. Washed her hands (step 1). She put on gloves (step 2) first instead.
  3. 10. 25 × 2 = 50; 50 − 10 = 40; 40 ÷ 4 = 10.
