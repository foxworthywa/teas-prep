Converting Units: Standard and Metric

TEAS objective M.2.5 · Measurement and Data

What the TEAS expects: you can convert within the metric system, within the U.S. standard (customary) system, and between the two, often in health-care settings. The TEAS usually gives the conversion factor for standard–metric conversions.

Calculator: the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it to multiply or divide by the conversion factor.

1. Within the metric system

Each prefix step is a factor of 10. Some common pairs skip steps: kilo to base, base to milli and milli to micro are each 3 steps (× 1,000); meters to centimeters is 2 steps (× 100). See S.4.1 for the full prefix table. The most common conversions:

Swipe the table sideways to see every column. →

Mass Volume Length
1 kg = 1,000 g 1 L = 1,000 mL 1 km = 1,000 m
1 g = 1,000 mg 1 mL = 1 cm³ (cc) 1 m = 100 cm
1 mg = 1,000 mcg (μg) 1 m = 1,000 mm

Smaller unit → bigger number: 0.5 g = 500 mg. Bigger unit → smaller number: 2,500 mL = 2.5 L.

2. Within the U.S. standard system

5,280 ft = 1 mi 8 fl oz = 1 cup
2 cups = 1 pt; 2 pt = 1 qt; 4 qt = 1 gal

3. Between systems (common approximations)

Standard Metric
1 in 2.54 cm
1 lb 0.454 kg (or 1 kg = 2.2 lb)
1 fl oz about 30 mL
1 tsp about 5 mL
1 tbsp about 15 mL
1 cup (8 fl oz) about 240 mL
1 qt about 0.95 L
1 mi about 1.61 km

4. The factor-label (dimensional analysis) method

Multiply by fractions equal to 1, arranged so the units you don't want cancel.

Example: convert 165 lb to kg. 165 lb × (1 kg / 2.2 lb) = 75 kg. (lb cancels.)

Two steps: convert 2 cups to mL. 2 cups × (8 fl oz / 1 cup) × (30 mL / 1 fl oz) = 480 mL.

Weight-based dose: 2 mg per kg for a 154-lb patient. 154 lb ÷ 2.2 = 70 kg; 70 × 2 mg = 140 mg.

Check 1: A baby is 24 inches long. How long is that in centimeters? Answer at the bottom of the page.

Check 2: A patient drinks 1.5 quarts of water. About how many milliliters is that? (Use 1 qt = 2 pt, 1 pt = 2 cups, 1 cup = 240 mL.) Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Multiplying when you should divide. kg is a bigger unit than lb, so the kg number is smaller.
  • Mixing fluid ounces (volume) with ounces (weight).
  • Skipping a step in a two-step conversion.
  • Forgetting to convert the patient's weight to kg before a per-kg dose.

Check answers

  1. 60.96 cm (about 61 cm). 24 × 2.54 = 60.96.
  2. 1,440 mL. 1.5 qt × 2 = 3 pt; 3 pt × 2 = 6 cups; 6 × 240 = 1,440 mL. (Using 1 qt ≈ 0.95 L gives about 1,425 mL; the small difference comes from rounding in the conversion factors.)
