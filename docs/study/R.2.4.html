TEAS objective R.2.4 · Craft and Structure

What the TEAS expects: you can identify who is telling a text (the narrator), how the author feels about the subject, and how the author's background shapes the view.

1. Narrative point of view

2. The author's attitude (tone)

Tone is the attitude an author shows toward the subject.

Word choice reveals how the author feels:

Attitude Clue words
Approving, enthusiastic excellent, promising, vital
Disapproving, critical ridiculous, careless, failure
Neutral, objective facts and plain description, few feeling words
Cautious, concerned the details matter, should study first, may, might

Check 1: "Later start times sound good, but the details matter." Is the author enthusiastic, opposed, or cautious? Answer at the bottom of the page.

3. Background shapes perspective

An author's job, experience, or interests affect what they notice and care about. A pediatrician writes about health; a bus company owner writes about buses and costs. Neither is "wrong"; each has a perspective.

Questions may ask:

  • Which statement would the author most likely agree with?
  • How does the author's background shape the argument?

To answer, list the author's main concerns, then match.

Check 2: A dentist writes an article about sports drinks. Which concern is she most likely to emphasize: (a) tooth decay from sugar, or (b) the price of the drinks? Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Confusing the author with a character in a story.
  • Deciding the author's view from one sentence. Look at the whole text.
  • Projecting your own opinion onto the author.
