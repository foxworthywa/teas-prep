TEAS objective M.1.9 · Number and Algebra

What the TEAS expects: you can write and simplify ratios, find unit rates, use distance-rate-time, and describe a rate of change.

Calculator: the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it for unit rates; convert minutes to hours first.

1. Ratios

A ratio compares two quantities: 12 to 18, 12:18 or 12/18.

  • Order matters. "Men to women" puts men first.
  • Simplify like a fraction: 12:18 → divide both by 6 → 2:3.
  • Part-to-part vs part-to-whole: with 12 men and 18 women, men to women is 2:3, but men to the whole class (30) is 12:30 = 2:5.

Splitting by a ratio: share $600 in the ratio 2:3. Total parts = 5; one part = $120. Shares: $240 and $360.

Check 1: A solution mixes concentrate and water in a 1:4 ratio. How much concentrate is in 500 mL of the solution? Answer at the bottom of the page.

2. Rates and unit rates

A rate compares quantities with different units: miles per hour, dollars per pound, drops per minute.

A unit rate has 1 in the denominator. Find it by dividing.

  • 150 miles in 2.5 hours → 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 miles per hour
  • $3.60 for 12 oz → 3.60 ÷ 12 = $0.30 per ounce

3. Distance = rate × time

d = r × t, so r = d ÷ t and t = d ÷ r.

Example: a trip of 195 miles at 65 mph takes 195 ÷ 65 = 3 hours.

Watch the units: at 60 mph for 45 minutes, convert 45 minutes to 0.75 hour → 60 × 0.75 = 45 miles.

4. Rate of change

Rate of change = change in output ÷ change in input (the slope of a line).

Example: a child weighed 18 kg at age 4 and 24 kg at age 7. Rate of change = (24 − 18) ÷ (7 − 4) = 6 ÷ 3 = 2 kg per year.

A positive rate means increasing; a negative rate means decreasing.

Check 2: An IV bag held 1,000 mL at 0800 and 640 mL at 1100. What was the average rate of change per hour? Answer at the bottom of the page.

Common traps

  • Reversing the order of a ratio. Write it in the order the question names.
  • Confusing part-to-part with part-to-whole.
  • Forgetting to convert minutes to hours in rate problems.
  • Dropping the sign of a rate of change. A decrease is negative.
