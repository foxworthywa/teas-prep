## Flagged items

**R.1.6-005 (two defensible answers).** The passage says Ms. Reyes "asked Marcus to bring flashlights from the supply closet *while* she checked that the backup generator had started. *Then* she asked him to sit with the patients." Checking the generator (B) happens at the same time as Marcus's errand, not "right after" the request. The next action that the text sequences with "Then" is C. A careful reader can defend either answer. Fix: change the stem to "What did Ms. Reyes do while Marcus brought the flashlights?" (key B). Or keep the stem, key C, and drop B.

**R.2.1-007 (giveaway).** The stem asks for a statement "from the passage." Only B appears in RP-17. A, C, and D are not in the passage at all, so a student can answer by matching text without telling fact from opinion. Fix: change the stem to "Which statement is a fact rather than an opinion?" Or rewrite the distractors so they are opinions drawn from or close to the passage.

**R.2.1-009 (outside reasoning; weak distractors).** The stem says "based on the Nutrition Facts label," but the label never mentions whole grains. The only link to the label is the 8 g of added sugars. That supports A only if the student already knows that added sugar argues against "healthy." B, C, and D are implausible. Fix: name the evidence in the stem (for example, "Which information on the label most challenges this slogan?" with the 8 g of added sugars as the key). Or give the distractors more pull.

**R.3.1-008 (misquote in a distractor).** Option C quotes "...resistant to antibiotics when they are overused." The passage says "when these drugs are overused." The option is wrong either way, but a quotation in an option should match the passage exactly.

**R.3.4-006 (unstated assumptions).** The answer of 7:35 a.m. (Run 1 at the Student Center) assumes three things the passages don't state. (1) The Campus Safety office is open at 7:35 a.m.; RP-11 gives no office hours. (2) January 10 is a weekday and not a college holiday; the shuttle runs Monday through Friday only. (3) The office is "next to the Student Center," which only the stem says; RP-11 places it in the Workforce Building. Item (3) is acceptable as stem information. Fix: add "The office opens at 7:30 a.m." and "January 10 is a Monday" to the stem, or ask only for the shuttle's arrival time.

**R.3.4-007 (second source not needed).** The answer (8 g ÷ 4 g per teaspoon ≈ 2 teaspoons) comes entirely from RP-23 plus the conversion in the stem. The letter (RP-06) only frames the question, so the item does not test integrating two sources. To make it a real integration item, have it use the letter's figures. Example: "What fraction of the AHA's daily limit for women is in one serving?" That gives 2 of 6 teaspoons, about one-third.

## Passage notes (RP-17 to RP-24)

**RP-17 Vaccines.** The facts are accurate at this level: memory cells after infection; vaccine types (weakened or killed germ, a harmless piece of it, mRNA-style instructions); flu vaccine updated yearly because the virus changes; common side effects; safety monitoring after approval. The serial comma is used ("a weakened or killed form of it, a harmless piece of it, or instructions..."). It reads at a suitable level and the tone is neutral. "Once the infection is over, the body keeps special cells called memory cells" is a fine simplification (the cells form during the response). No changes needed.

**RP-18 Fire extinguisher.** PASS is correct, and so are the details: the pin breaks the tamper seal; aim at the base of the fire; squeeze slowly and evenly; sweep side to side; start 6 to 8 ft away (consistent with common OSHA and fire-service guidance); watch for re-ignition; report the fire. One safety point: standard guidance says to pull the alarm or make sure 911 has been called *before* fighting even a small fire. As written, calling 911 is mentioned only for a spreading or smoky fire, plus reporting afterward. Suggest adding "make sure someone has called 911 or pulled the fire alarm" to the pre-check list. This does not affect R.1.3-005. Serial commas are present.

**RP-19 Shuttle.** Internally consistent: 15, 10, and 10 minute intervals in every run. Clear. Note for R.3.4-006: the day of the week and holidays matter, and no dates are given.

**RP-20 The Storm Shift.** Consistent and suitable, with good inference material. The one sequence ambiguity ("while... Then") is noted under R.1.6-005. The contraction "didn't" is fine in narrative.

**RP-21 Phones.** Balanced, clear, and suitable. The serial comma is present ("more discussion, fewer missing assignments, and fewer conflicts"). No issues.

**RP-22 Walking.** (1) The title calls it "A Survey of One Workplace," but the data are step-counter records, not a survey. Suggest "A Walking Challenge at One Workplace." (2) Logic: the table shows the average daily steps *per participant*. Fewer people taking part does not by itself lower a per-participant average. The intended meaning is that employees on vacation logged fewer steps (or stopped wearing counters). Suggest: "...noted that many employees took vacations in May and logged fewer steps." This would also tighten R.1.2-007, R.2.3-009, and R.3.1-007. The numbers are consistent: the goal of 7,000 is first met in April (7,400), and May (7,200) is still above it.

**RP-23 Nutrition label.** (1) Internal inconsistency: the final paragraph explains % Daily Value, but the label shows no %DV figures. Either add a %DV column (for example, sodium 8%, fiber 14%, added sugars 16%) or delete that paragraph. (2) Real labels also list saturated fat, cholesterol, and so on. Leaving them out is an acceptable simplification for a fictional product. (3) Calories are plausible (about 158 kcal from macronutrients, which allowing for fiber fits 150). (4) The standard wording is "Includes 8 g Added Sugars." The current wording is fine.

**RP-24 Penicillin.** Checked against the history: Fleming, a Scottish scientist, found the mold in 1928 after a vacation (correct). He published in 1929 (correct). He could not purify or produce enough to treat patients (correct). Florey and Chain led the Oxford work around 1940, with mice in 1940 and the first human patients in 1941 (correct at the "around 1940" level). Large-scale wartime production by US drug companies is correct, though production also happened in Britain; "especially in the United States" would be more precise but is optional. The 1945 Nobel Prize shared by Fleming, Florey, and Chain is correct.
- **Accuracy nuance to fix:** the passage says doctors warn about resistance when antibiotics are *overused*, and that "Fleming himself gave the same warning in his Nobel lecture." Fleming's 1945 lecture actually warned about *underdosing*: patients taking too little penicillin could expose microbes to non-lethal amounts and make them resistant. That is a warning about misuse, not overuse as such. Suggest: "Fleming himself warned in his Nobel lecture that misusing penicillin, for example by taking too little, could make bacteria resistant." Item R.1.2-008 (key C) still works with this wording.
- Serial commas are present ("Fleming, Florey, and Chain"). The readability and tone are suitable.
