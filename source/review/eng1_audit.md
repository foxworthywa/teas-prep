# English Batch 1 Audit (eng_batch1.json, 32 items)

Auditor scope: I checked every key against standard American English and looked for a second defensible answer. I checked the grammar terms in explanations, whether each distractor note matches its letter and diagnoses the error correctly, the medical word parts, objective fit, and clueing. The file was not edited.

## Bottom line

- **Keys:** All 32 keys are correct. No item has a second defensible answer under standard American English. The serial-comma item (E.1.2-004) avoids style dependence because no option is the correct no-serial-comma version.
- **Must-fix:** none.

- **Medical word parts:** All are standard (hyper-, hypo-, intra-/endo-, pre-, cardi/o, -logy, -itis, -ectomy, -tomy, -algia).

## Bank-wide note: serial comma consistency (should-fix)

E.1.2-004 says the serial comma "is standard in most academic writing." But the bank's own explanations and options leave it out in at least these places:
- E.1.1-004 explanation ("definitely ... and accommodate")
- E.1.3-004 note ("B, C and D")
- E.2.1-001 note ("A, C and D")
- E.2.3-003 note ("A, B and D")
- E.3.1-001 explanation ("chooses a focus and organizes")
- E.3.1-001 note ("grammar, spelling and punctuation")
- E.3.1-002 explanation ("grammar and capitalization") and note ("organization and clarity")
- E.3.1-003 explanation ("moving, adding or cutting")
- E.3.1-004 option B ("reading, math, science and English") and its note

A student who reads closely will see that the bank breaks its own rule. **Fix:** use the serial comma throughout all student-facing text. Also add one sentence to E.1.2-004: "Some styles, such as AP (newspaper) style, leave it out; both are acceptable, but be consistent." That way students do not mark correct non-serial lists as wrong elsewhere.
