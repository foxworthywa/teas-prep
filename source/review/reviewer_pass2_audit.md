## D. Study pages

### S.1.1_body-orientation.md: no factual errors

I checked every section and found them correct: anatomical position, the directional pairs and their examples, supine/prone, the plane table and the coronal memory aid, the cavities (pleural cavities, mediastinum contents, abdominal and pelvic organs, retroperitoneal kidneys), serous membranes (parietal/visceral, serous fluid, pleura/pericardium/peritoneum), the four quadrants and their organs, the nine regions (epigastric/umbilical/hypogastric; hypochondriac/lumbar/iliac-inguinal), the appendicitis and gallbladder notes, and all check answers.

- FLAG (optional clarity, not an error), line 79: "pericardium (heart)" is listed as a serous membrane. The pericardial sac also has an outer *fibrous* layer that is not serous. The S.1.3 page uses "pericardium" for the whole sac, and its "epicardium" is the visceral layer of the serous pericardium. Suggested addition: "...*pericardium* (heart; its visceral layer is the epicardium, the outer layer of the heart wall)." This ties the two pages together.

### S.1.3_cardiovascular-system.md: no outright errors; two overstatements to soften

I checked these and found them correct: the pericardium and the epicardium/myocardium/endocardium layers, coronary arteries as the first branches of the aorta, the path of blood, LAB RAT, AV versus semilunar valves, heart sounds (lub = AV valves closing at the start of ventricular contraction; dub = semilunar valves closing as the ventricles relax), the exceptions to the artery/vein oxygen rule (pulmonary and umbilical), vessel order, one-cell-thick capillaries, the artery/vein table (including "Valves: No" for arteries, and "many veins, especially in the limbs" have valves), systolic/diastolic blood pressure, the conduction pathway and its apex-to-base contraction, plasma about 55% and formed elements about 45%, anucleate red blood cells, platelets as clotting fragments, all formed elements from red marrow in adults, and all check answers.

- FIX (overstatement), line 20: "Blood passing through the chambers does not nourish the heart muscle." The innermost layer of the wall does receive some nutrients by diffusion from chamber blood. Change to: "Blood passing through the chambers cannot nourish the thick heart muscle."
- FIX (precision), line 20: "A blocked coronary artery starves part of the muscle; that is a heart attack." Change to: "A blocked coronary artery starves part of the muscle; if the blockage lasts, that muscle dies. That is a heart attack (myocardial infarction)." A heart attack is the death of muscle, not the blockage itself, and nursing applicants will meet "MI" immediately.
