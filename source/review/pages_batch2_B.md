## S.1.8 The Integumentary System

1. **ERROR (clarity that could cause a wrong answer): the section heading and a Common Trap contradict each other.**
   The section heading says "## 1. Three layers" and lists the hypodermis as layer 3. The trap then says: "**Calling the hypodermis part of the skin's two layers.** The skin proper is epidermis plus dermis; the hypodermis lies beneath." The trap's wording doesn't parse ("part of the skin's two layers"), and it tells students not to count the hypodermis right after the page counted it. Many TEAS-style items list "epidermis, dermis, hypodermis (subcutaneous)" together as layers of the integument. A student who takes this trap literally may reject a correct answer.
   Fix:
   - Heading: replace `## 1. Three layers` with `## 1. Two skin layers, plus the layer beneath`
   - Trap: replace the whole bullet with: `- **Getting the layer count confused.** The skin itself is the epidermis plus the dermis. The hypodermis (subcutaneous layer) lies just beneath the skin and is often listed with them as a third layer. Know all three and what each contains.`

2. **IMPROVE (coverage): name the tissue type of the epidermis.** "a stack of epithelial cells" → "**stratified squamous epithelium** (a stack of flat epithelial cells)". Questions often ask which tissue makes up the epidermis.

3. **IMPROVE (coverage): name the main epidermal cell.** After "fill with the tough protein **keratin**", add: "(these cells are called **keratinocytes**)".

4. **IMPROVE (accuracy/linkage): vitamin D.** "UV light starts the conversion of a cholesterol-based molecule in the skin into vitamin D, which the body needs to absorb calcium." This is correct but incomplete: the skin makes an inactive form. Replace with: "UV light starts the conversion of a cholesterol-based molecule in the skin into an inactive form of vitamin D; the liver and kidneys then activate it. Active vitamin D helps the intestine absorb calcium." This also matches the urinary page ("Activate vitamin D").

5. **IMPROVE (minor coverage): dermis sublayers.** Optionally add after the dermis description: "It has a thin upper **papillary layer** (with the ridges that make fingerprints) and a thick deeper **reticular layer**."

6. **OK:** Avascular epidermis, stratum basale/corneum, melanocytes and melanin, dermis contents, hypodermis fat, thermoregulation (dilate/sweat vs. constrict), sensation, excretion, eccrine vs. apocrine (location, puberty, odor from bacterial breakdown), sebaceous glands, arrector pili, nails: all correct. Both Check questions and answers are correct and unambiguous (free nerve endings do reach the deep epidermis, so the "stings" explanation holds). **Figure 1** (OpenStax 5.2) matches its alt text and caption; the brackets in the image label epidermis, dermis and hypodermis as described; it fits the section.

IMPROVE count: 4

## S.1.10 The Urinary System

1. **ERROR (misleading clinical statement): "Large amounts of protein or blood in the urine suggest the filter is damaged."**
   This holds for protein. It does not hold for blood: blood in the urine (hematuria) often comes from below the glomerulus (kidney stones, urinary tract infection, bladder or prostate disease, trauma). Nursing applicants should not learn "blood in urine = damaged filter."
   Fix: replace the sentence with: "Large amounts of **protein** in the urine suggest the glomerular filter is damaged. **Blood** in the urine can come from a damaged filter or from anywhere along the urinary tract (for example, a kidney stone or an infection)."

2. **IMPROVE (coverage; high priority): name the nephron segments.** The page never names the proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle, or distal convoluted tubule. Questions often ask about the route of filtrate or where most reabsorption happens. Replace "**Reabsorption** along the tubule:" with "**Reabsorption** along the tubule, which runs in order: **proximal convoluted tubule** (where most reabsorption happens) → **loop of Henle** (helps concentrate urine) → **distal convoluted tubule** → collecting duct." Optionally add a nephron figure (OpenStax 25.11 or similar).

3. **IMPROVE (absolute wording): glucose in urine.** "glucose in the urine means blood glucose is so high…" → "glucose in the urine **usually** means blood glucose is so high…" (there are other causes, such as pregnancy and rare kidney tubule disorders).

4. **IMPROVE (clarity): where urine is finalized.** "What is left flows into a **collecting duct** as urine." This reads as if urine is finished before it reaches the collecting duct, but Section 3 then says ADH acts on the collecting ducts. Replace with: "What is left flows into a **collecting duct**, where the final amount of water reabsorbed is adjusted (by ADH), and leaves as urine."

5. **IMPROVE (coverage): EPO trigger.** "…and **erythropoietin (EPO)**, which tells the red bone marrow…" → "…and, when blood oxygen is low, **erythropoietin (EPO)**, which tells the red bone marrow…". Pairs with the renin trigger already given.

6. **IMPROVE (coverage): retroperitoneal.** After "They sit against the back wall of the abdomen" add "(behind the peritoneum, called **retroperitoneal**)".

7. **IMPROVE (precision): path of urine.** "calyces" → "minor and major calyces" so the path matches Figure 1's labels.

8. **OK:** Organs and their counts; peristalsis in the ureters; short female urethra and UTIs; cortex, medulla, pyramids, pelvis; filtration (blood pressure; size-based; cells and most proteins retained); all glucose reabsorbed normally; secretion of H+ and K+; ADH (and alcohol suppressing it); aldosterone; renin; kidney functions list: all correct. Both Check questions are correct and unambiguous. **Figure 1** (OpenStax 25.8): the image shows cortex, medulla, pyramids, papilla, minor and major calyces, renal pelvis, ureter, hilum with renal artery, vein and nerve, and an inset of kidney position, matching the alt text and caption.
