## S.1.2 The Respiratory System

1. **ERROR: path of air, step 1.** The page says: "**Nose (nasal cavity)** or mouth: air is warmed, moistened and filtered." This credits the mouth with filtering. The mouth does little filtering; the nasal hairs, mucus and turbinates of the nasal cavity do it. A TEAS item that asks "which structure filters inhaled air" expects the nose. Exact fix: "1. **Nose (nasal cavity):** air is warmed, moistened and filtered by hairs and sticky mucus. (Air can also enter through the mouth, but the mouth does not filter it well.)"
2. **IMPROVE: conducting zone sentence.** The page says: "Everything from the nose to the bronchioles is the **conducting zone** ... The alveoli (and the smallest bronchioles that bear them) are the **respiratory zone**." This puts the bronchioles in both zones in back-to-back sentences. Fix: "Everything from the nose to the **terminal bronchioles** is the conducting zone: it only moves air. The **respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts and alveoli** are the respiratory zone, where gas exchange happens." This also matches the labels in Figure 2.
3. **IMPROVE: coverage of gas exchange in the tissues.** Check 2 asks about oxygen diffusion in leg muscle, but section 2 only teaches exchange in the lungs. Fix: add a bullet to section 2: "**In the body's tissues the directions reverse:** oxygen diffuses from the blood into the cells (cells use it up), and CO₂ diffuses from the cells into the blood. Most CO₂ travels in the blood as **bicarbonate**."
4. **IMPROVE: coverage of the lungs themselves.** TEAS items can hinge on lobes, pleura, and cilia/mucus. Fix: add after step 7: "The **right lung has 3 lobes; the left has 2** (making room for the heart). Each lung is wrapped in a two-layer **pleura** with a thin film of fluid between the layers. **Cilia** and **mucus** lining the airways trap particles and sweep them up toward the throat."
5. **IMPROVE: forced exhalation.** Add to the "Exhalation" block: "During hard breathing (exercise, coughing), exhalation becomes active: the **internal intercostals** and **abdominal muscles** contract to push air out."
6. **OK:** Larynx and epiglottis, C-shaped tracheal rings, bronchioles without cartilage, diffusion down partial-pressure gradients, hemoglobin carrying most O₂, surfactant, inspiration and expiration mechanics, Boyle's law, brainstem control, and CO₂ as the main drive are all accurate. The common traps are correct.
7. **OK, figures:**
   - Fig 1 (22.2) labels match the alt text exactly; the caption is accurate.
   - Fig 2 (22.10) matches the alt text: terminal bronchiole, respiratory bronchiole, alveolar duct and sac, capillaries, deoxygenated blood from the pulmonary artery, and oxygenated blood to the pulmonary vein.
   - Fig 3 (22.17) matches the alt text and caption.
   - All three figures are placed appropriately.
8. **OK, checks and answers:** all three are correct and unambiguous.

## S.1.4 The Digestive System

1. **ERROR: accessory organs list and definition.** The page says: "The **accessory organs** help but food never passes through them: **salivary glands, liver, gallbladder and pancreas.**" The OpenStax section this page links to (23.1) lists the accessory organs as **teeth, tongue, salivary glands, liver, gallbladder and pancreas**. Most TEAS prep sources list them the same way. With the page's "food never passes through them" definition, a student would reject "tongue" or "teeth" as an accessory organ and miss the item. Exact fix: "The **accessory organs** help digestion but are not part of the tube itself: the **teeth and tongue** in the mouth, plus the **salivary glands, liver, gallbladder and pancreas**. Food never passes through the glands, liver, gallbladder or pancreas; they send their secretions into the tract through ducts." The Figure 1 caption sentence ("The liver, gallbladder and pancreas are accessory organs: food never passes through them") remains true and can stay.
2. **IMPROVE: Figure 1 shows the spleen.** The alt text lists the spleen among the digestive structures, and nothing on the page says it is not digestive. A reader can conclude that the spleen is a digestive organ. Fix: add to the caption: "The spleen is shown only for position; it belongs to the lymphatic/immune system, not the digestive system." Also add "anal canal" to the alt text, since it is labeled in the image.
3. **IMPROVE: table, "Where digestion starts" for fats.** The entry "Mostly small intestine" does not answer "where does it start," and a small amount of fat digestion does start earlier (lingual and gastric lipase). Fix: "Small intestine (where almost all of it happens)".
4. **IMPROVE: coverage of the sphincters.** These are plausible TEAS items. Fix: add to step 4: "A ring of muscle at each end controls flow: the **lower esophageal (cardiac) sphincter** keeps acid out of the esophagus; the **pyloric sphincter** lets chyme into the duodenum a little at a time." Optionally, add to the stomach entry: "the acid (HCl) kills microbes and activates pepsin."
5. **IMPROVE: where absorbed nutrients go.** Add to section 4: "Blood from the intestines goes first to the **liver** through the **hepatic portal vein**, which is why the liver 'processes absorbed nutrients.'"
6. **IMPROVE: wording "each villus cell has microvilli."** Fix: "the cells covering each villus have **microvilli**."
7. **OK:** The order of the tract, salivary amylase, pepsin and trypsin, chyme, mechanical and chemical digestion, bile (emulsifies, no enzymes), gallbladder storage, pancreatic enzymes plus bicarbonate, lacteals for fat, large-intestine water absorption and vitamin K, the stomach's limited absorption, and the colon order are all accurate.
8. **OK, figure:** Fig 1 (23.2) labels match the alt text, apart from item 2 above. Its placement is appropriate.
9. **OK, checks and answers:** both are correct. The gallbladder answer is well reasoned.

## S.1.5 The Nervous System

1. **ERROR: sympathetic and parasympathetic table, blood glucose row.** The page says the parasympathetic effect on blood glucose is "No direct effect". This is false. Vagal (parasympathetic) stimulation of the pancreas increases **insulin** release and favors glucose storage. Exact fix: change the parasympathetic cell to "Favors storage (stimulates insulin release)". Alternatively, delete the row. TEAS does not need it, and the sympathetic half is already covered by "fuel to muscles."
2. **IMPROVE: coverage of how a signal is made.** A basic action potential question is plausible at TEAS depth. Fix: add to section 2: "The signal along the axon is an **action potential**: sodium (Na⁺) rushes into the cell (**depolarization**), then potassium (K⁺) flows out (**repolarization**). At the synapse the signal becomes chemical (neurotransmitter)."
3. **IMPROVE: Figure 1 alt text and caption.** The image labels an **oligodendrocyte** making the myelin, which means it shows a CNS neuron. It also labels "Synapse" but not "axon terminals." Fix the alt text: "...a long axon wrapped in segments of myelin sheath made by an oligodendrocyte, with nodes of Ranvier between them, and branching axon terminals ending at a synapse." Optionally add to the caption: "Here the myelin is made by an oligodendrocyte, so this is a CNS neuron."
4. **IMPROVE: spinal cord and protection.** Fix: add one bullet to section 1: "The brain and spinal cord are protected by bone, three membranes (**meninges**) and **cerebrospinal fluid**. The spinal cord carries signals between the brain and body and handles simple reflexes."
5. **OK:** The CNS and PNS split, afferent and efferent with the A/E memory aid, somatic and autonomic, the parts of the neuron and the direction of signal flow, myelin and saltatory conduction, Schwann cells and oligodendrocytes, glia, the five-part reflex arc, the sympathetic and parasympathetic rows other than item 1 (including NE and ACh), and the brain-region and lobe tables are all accurate.
6. **OK, Figure 2 (original reflex arc):** it matches the alt text step for step. The caption claim that the hand moves before pain is felt is the standard and defensible teaching. The figure is appropriate.
7. **OK, checks and answers:** both are correct and unambiguous.

## S.1.6 The Muscular System

1. **IMPROVE: coverage of body movements.** The objective says "describe how muscles produce movement," and TEAS items can name the movement types. Fix: add to section 4: "Movement words: **flexion** (decrease the joint angle) versus **extension** (increase it); **abduction** (move away from the midline) versus **adduction** (move toward it); **rotation**."
2. **IMPROVE: motor units and contraction types.** Fix: add to section 4: "A **motor unit** is one motor neuron plus all the fibers it controls; more units recruited means more force. In **isotonic** contraction the muscle changes length and moves a load; in **isometric** contraction tension rises but length does not change (holding a plank)."
3. **IMPROVE: table, cardiac nucleus count.** Fix: "Branched, usually one nucleus (sometimes two), joined by **intercalated discs**". Optionally add a row "Sarcomeres: Yes / Yes / No (not arranged in sarcomeres)" to explain why smooth muscle has no stripes.
4. **IMPROVE: step 6, relaxation.** Fix: "When the nerve signal stops, acetylcholine is broken down (by acetylcholinesterase), calcium is pumped back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum, tropomyosin covers actin again, and the muscle relaxes."
5. **OK:** The three tissue types (control, striations, location), the purpose of intercalated discs, the structural hierarchy, actin and myosin and Z discs, the excitation-contraction steps (ACh, Ca²⁺ from the SR, troponin and tropomyosin, cross-bridge cycling with ATP), filaments not shortening, ATP and rigor mortis, muscles only pulling, agonist and antagonist (biceps and triceps), tendon versus ligament, origin and insertion, and heat production are all accurate at TEAS depth. The common traps are correct.
6. **OK, figure:** Fig 1 (10.10) matches the alt text exactly: in the contracted panel the Z discs are closer and the I band and H zone narrower, while the A band is unchanged. The caption is accurate and the placement is appropriate.
7. **OK, checks and answers:** both are correct and unambiguous.

## S.1.7 The Reproductive System

1. **IMPROVE: Figure 2 has two incorrect labels inherited from OpenStax.** The lateral view labels "**Posterior fornix of uterus**"; the fornix belongs to the **vagina**. It also labels "**Labium minora**" and "**Labium majora**", which mixes the Latin singular and plural; the correct forms are labia minora/majora or labium minus/majus. Neither label is likely to be tested, but both are false text on a public page. Fix (pick one): add a caption note ("Note: the label 'posterior fornix of uterus' should read 'posterior fornix of the vagina'"), crop the figure to panel (b) only, or correct the labels in an edited copy (CC BY permits this; mark it "modified").
2. **IMPROVE: the objective promises "trace the path ... of the egg," but no explicit path is given.** Fix: add at the top of section 2: "**Path of the egg:** ovary → released at **ovulation** → swept in by the **fimbriae** → **uterine tube** (where fertilization happens) → **uterus** (where the embryo implants)."
3. **IMPROVE: hormone coverage.** Labor contractions and the timing of the cycle are plausible TEAS items. Fix: add a table row "**Oxytocin** | Posterior pituitary (made in hypothalamus) | Uterine contractions during labor; milk let-down". Add a sentence under the table: "In a typical 28-day cycle, ovulation happens around **day 14**. **GnRH** from the hypothalamus drives the pituitary to release FSH and LH."
4. **IMPROVE: implantation wording.** The page says "The zygote divides as it travels down the uterine tube and implants...". By implantation it is no longer a zygote. Fix: "The zygote divides as it travels down the uterine tube; about a week after fertilization the resulting ball of cells (a **blastocyst**) implants in the endometrium."
5. **OK:** The seminiferous tubules and Leydig cells, the epididymis, vas deferens, ejaculatory duct and urethra, the accessory glands (seminal vesicles and fructose), scrotal temperature, the ovaries' hormones, the uterine tube as the site of fertilization, the endometrium and myometrium, the cervix and vagina, meiosis and 23/46 chromosomes, and the hormone table (FSH, LH surge, estrogen, progesterone from the corpus luteum, testosterone, hCG and pregnancy tests) are all accurate. The menstruation explanation and the common traps are correct.
6. **OK, Figure 1 (27.2):** it matches the alt text and caption. "Penis" is labeled in the external panel (b), and the urethra is labeled by segment (prostatic, membranous, spongy). The figure is appropriate.
7. **OK, checks and answers:** both are correct and unambiguous.
