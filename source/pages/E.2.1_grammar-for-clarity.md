# Grammar for Clear Writing

**TEAS objective E.2.1** · Knowledge of Language

**What the TEAS expects:** you can make subjects and verbs agree, choose the right pronoun, keep verb tense consistent, and avoid unclear pronoun references.

---

> **Words you need**
> - **Subject:** who or what the sentence is about. **Verb:** the action or state (*runs, is, seems*).
> - **Pronoun:** a word that stands in for a noun (*she, it, they, him*). Its **antecedent** is the noun it stands for: in *The nurses brought their badges*, "nurses" is the antecedent of "their."
> - **Preposition:** a small word that shows position or relationship (*of, in, on, between, to, with, for*).
> - **First person** = the speaker (*I, we*); **second person** = the one spoken to (*you*); **third person** = everyone else (*he, she, it, they*).

---

## 1. Subject–verb agreement

Singular subject → singular verb; plural subject → plural verb.

| Tricky case | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Words between subject and verb | Ignore them; find the true subject | The **box** of gloves **is** empty. |
| each, every, either, neither, anyone, everybody (used alone) | **Singular** | **Each** of the students **has** a badge. **Neither** of the doors **is** locked. |
| Subjects joined by **and** | Plural | Maria and Jo **are** here. |
| Pairs with **or / nor** (either…or, neither…nor) | Verb agrees with the subject **nearer to it** | Neither the nurses nor the **doctor was** late. Neither the doctor nor the **nurses were** late. |
| **some, all, none, most, any** | Depends on the noun after "of" | **Some** of the **water is** gone. **Some** of the **cups are** gone. (Some tests still treat *none* as always singular: *None of the cups is…*) |
| Collective nouns (team, staff, class) | Usually singular in American English | The **staff is** meeting at noon. |
| Sentences starting with **There is / There are** | The subject comes **after** the verb | There **is** one **chart** on the desk. There **are** two **charts**. |

> **Check 1:** Choose the verb: "The list of patients (is / are) on the desk."

## 2. Pronoun case

| | Subject form (does the action) | Object form (receives the action, or follows a preposition) |
|---|---|---|
| First person | I, we | me, us |
| Second person | you | you |
| Third person | he, she, they | him, her, them |
| Questions | who | whom |

- **Test by removing the other person:** "Maria and (I / me) went." → "I went" ✓.
- After prepositions use object forms: "between you and **me**."
- **Myself** must refer back to *I* or *me* earlier in the sentence: "I hurt myself," "I did it myself." It can't replace *I* or *me*: "Maria and myself went" ✗.
- **Who or whom?** Answer the question with he or him. *(Who/Whom) called?* → **He** called → **who**. *(Who/Whom) did you call?* → I called **him** → **whom**.

---

## 3. Pronoun–antecedent agreement and clarity

- A pronoun must **match its antecedent in number**: one noun → singular pronoun; more than one → plural. *The **nurses** brought **their** badges.* *The **nurse** brought **her** badge.*
- A pronoun must clearly refer to one noun. *When Dana called Kim, she was upset.* (Who was upset?) ✗ → *Dana was upset when she called Kim.* ✓
- Singular "they" is now widely accepted for a person whose gender is unknown or who uses they: *Each student should bring their ID.* Some formal styles still prefer *his or her*. On a test, the safest choice is often an answer that makes the subject plural so "their" clearly fits: *Students should bring their IDs.*

## 4. Consistent verb tense

Don't shift tense without a reason. *She opened the chart and reads the notes.* ✗ → *She opened the chart and read the notes.* ✓

## 5. Adjectives vs adverbs

Adjectives describe nouns; adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. *She did* ***well*** *on the test* (not *good*). *The patient feels* ***bad*** (not *badly*). **Linking verbs** (*is, seems, feels, looks, becomes*) connect the subject to a word that describes it, so they take an adjective. Test: if you can swap in "is" (*The patient is bad*), use the adjective.

---

## 6. Clear, direct sentences

- **Active voice** (the subject does the action) is usually clearer than **passive voice** (the subject receives it). *The nurse gave the medication* (active) vs *The medication was given by the nurse* (passive). Passive is acceptable when the doer is unknown or unimportant, as in many lab reports.
- **Cut wordiness:** *due to the fact that* → *because*; *at this point in time* → *now*; *in order to* → *to*.
- **Fewer vs less:** *fewer* for things you can count (fewer patients); *less* for amounts (less water).

---

## Common traps

- **Matching the verb to the nearest noun** instead of the subject.
- **"Me and her went."** Use subject forms in the subject.
- **Unclear "it," "this," or "they."** Name the noun if two could fit.
