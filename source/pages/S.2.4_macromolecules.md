# Biological Macromolecules

**TEAS objective S.2.4** · Biology

**What the TEAS expects:** you can name the four classes of biological macromolecules, their building blocks (monomers), their main jobs and examples, and describe how enzymes work.

---

## 1. The big four

Large biological molecules are mostly **polymers**: long chains of repeating small units called **monomers**.

| Macromolecule | Building block (monomer) | Main jobs | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | **Monosaccharides** (simple sugars such as glucose) | Quick energy; structure | Glucose, starch (plant storage), glycogen (animal storage, in liver and muscle), cellulose (plant cell walls) |
| **Lipids** | Glycerol and **fatty acids** (for fats); lipids are not true polymers | Long-term energy storage, membranes, insulation, hormones | Triglycerides (fats and oils), phospholipids, steroids such as cholesterol and estrogen |
| **Proteins** | **Amino acids** (20 kinds), joined by peptide bonds | Enzymes, structure, transport, defense, signals | Enzymes, hemoglobin, antibodies, collagen, keratin, insulin |
| **Nucleic acids** | **Nucleotides** | Store and carry genetic information | DNA, RNA (ATP is a modified nucleotide that carries energy) |

**Carbohydrate terms:** a **monosaccharide** is one sugar unit (glucose, fructose); a **disaccharide** is two (sucrose = table sugar, lactose = milk sugar); a **polysaccharide** is many (starch, glycogen, cellulose).

**Fats:** **saturated** fats have no double bonds in their fatty acid chains and are solid at room temperature (butter); **unsaturated** fats have one or more double bonds and are liquid (olive oil).

**Elements:** carbohydrates and lipids are mostly C, H and O. Proteins add **nitrogen** (and often sulfur). Nucleic acids contain C, H, O, N and **phosphorus**.

## 2. Building up and breaking down

- **Dehydration synthesis:** monomers are joined into a polymer and a **water molecule is removed** at each bond.
- **Hydrolysis:** a polymer is split into monomers by **adding water**. Digestion is hydrolysis.

**Memory hook:** *hydro-lysis* = water splits it.

---

## 3. Energy per gram

- Fats: about **9 Calories per gram**.
- Carbohydrates and proteins: about **4 Calories per gram**.

Fat stores more than twice the energy per gram, which is why the body stores long-term energy as fat. Carbohydrates are the quickest fuel.

---

## 4. Phospholipids and membranes

A phospholipid has a **hydrophilic** (water-loving) phosphate head and two **hydrophobic** (water-fearing) fatty acid tails. In water they form a **bilayer**: heads face the watery inside and outside of the cell, tails hide in the middle. This bilayer is the basic structure of every cell membrane.

---

## 5. Enzymes

- Most enzymes are **proteins**. Their names often end in **-ase** (lactase, amylase, lipase).
- They are **catalysts**: they speed up reactions by **lowering the activation energy** (the energy needed to get a reaction started), and they are **not used up**.
- The molecule an enzyme acts on is its **substrate**. It fits into the enzyme's **active site**, which has a specific shape.
- **Denaturation:** high temperature or the wrong pH changes the enzyme's shape, so the substrate no longer fits and the enzyme stops working. Each enzyme has an optimum: most human enzymes work best near 37 °C; pepsin in the stomach works best at an acidic pH near 2. **Cold** slows enzymes down but does not denature them; activity returns when they warm up.

## Common traps

- **Matching the wrong monomer.** Amino acids build proteins; nucleotides build nucleic acids; monosaccharides build carbohydrates.
- **Saying enzymes are used up.** They are reused.
- **Thinking all lipids are fats.** Steroids and phospholipids are lipids too.
- **Mixing up glycogen and glucose.** Glucose is the monomer; glycogen is the animal storage polymer.
