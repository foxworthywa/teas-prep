# Comparing and Ordering Numbers

**TEAS objective M.1.3** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can put a mix of fractions, decimals, percents and negative numbers in order from least to greatest (or the reverse).


**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it to turn each fraction into a decimal.

---

## 1. The reliable method: convert everything to decimals

1. Change every number to a decimal (fractions: divide; percents: move the point 2 left).
2. Line up the decimal points and add zeros so they have the same number of places.
3. Compare digit by digit from the left.
4. Write the answer using the **original** forms.

**Example:** order 2/3, 0.6, 65%, 0.66
- 2/3 ≈ 0.667 → 0.667
- 0.6 → 0.600
- 65% → 0.650
- 0.66 → 0.660
- Least to greatest: **0.6, 65%, 0.66, 2/3**

> **Check 1:** Order from least to greatest: 3/4, 0.7, 72%, 0.745.

## 2. Comparing fractions without decimals

- **Same denominator:** bigger numerator is bigger (5/8 > 3/8).
- **Same numerator:** **smaller** denominator is bigger (1/3 > 1/4, because the pieces are bigger).
- **Cross-multiply:** to compare 5/7 and 7/10, multiply each numerator by the *other* fraction's denominator. 5/7 gets 5 × 10 = 50; 7/10 gets 7 × 7 = 49. 50 > 49, so **5/7 > 7/10**.

---

## 3. Negative numbers

On a number line, numbers **increase to the right**. So:
- Any negative number is less than 0 and less than any positive number.
- Among negatives, the one **farther from zero is smaller**: −8 < −3.
- −0.5 > −0.75 (−0.5 is closer to zero).

**Symbols:** < means "is less than"; > means "is greater than." The symbol opens toward the bigger number.

## Common traps

- **Comparing the number of digits.** 0.45 > 0.405 even though 0.405 has more digits. Add zeros: 0.450 vs 0.405.
- **Thinking a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction.** 1/8 < 1/5.
- **Ordering negatives like positives.** −10 is less than −2.
- **Answering in converted form.** Give the answer in the forms the question used.
