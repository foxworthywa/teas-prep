# Multi-Step Word Problems

**TEAS objective M.1.5** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can solve real-world problems that take one or several arithmetic steps: totals, differences, unit prices, rates and leftovers.


**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it for every multi-digit step, and estimate first so you notice a slip.

---

## 1. A four-step plan

1. **Read** the whole problem and underline **what is asked**, with its units.
2. **List** the numbers you're given, with their units. Cross out information you don't need.
3. **Plan** the steps: what must you know first to get the answer?
4. **Solve and check:** does the answer make sense? Is it in the right units?

---

## 2. Key words (clues, not rules)

| Operation | Common clue words |
|---|---|
| Add | total, sum, altogether, combined, increased by |
| Subtract | difference, how many more, left, remaining, decreased by |
| Multiply | each, per (when finding a total), times, of (with fractions/percents) |
| Divide | per (when finding a rate), split, shared equally, average, each (when finding one share) |

Clue words can mislead. Always ask what the situation actually requires.

---

## 3. Worked examples

**Total and remainder:** A nurse has a 1,000 mL bag. She infuses 125 mL per hour for 6 hours. How much is left?
- Used: 125 × 6 = 750 mL
- Left: 1,000 − 750 = **250 mL**

**Unit price (best buy):** 12 cans for $9.00, or 18 cans for $12.60. Which is cheaper per can?
- $9.00 ÷ 12 = $0.75 per can; $12.60 ÷ 18 = $0.70 per can → **the 18-pack**

**Rounding up in context:** 74 students go on a trip; each van holds 12. How many vans?
- 74 ÷ 12 = 6.17 → **7 vans** (you can't take part of a van, and 6 vans leave 2 students behind)

> **Check 1:** A student earns $14.50 an hour and works 22 hours a week. Her weekly expenses are $260. How much does she have left each week?

## 4. Time and money

- Minutes to hours: divide by 60 (90 minutes = 1.5 hours).
- Hours to minutes: multiply by 60 (0.25 hour = 15 minutes).
- **24-hour clock** (used in health care): times run from 0000 (midnight) to 2359. For p.m. hours, **add 12**: 2:00 p.m. = 1400; 8:30 p.m. = 2030. After 2359 the clock restarts at 0000. Elapsed time = later time − earlier time (0800 to 1100 = 3 hours).
- Elapsed time: count up to the next whole hour, then to the end time. 8:45 to 11:20 = 15 min + 2 h + 20 min = **2 h 35 min**.

> **Check 2:** A medication is given every 6 hours, starting at 0800 (8:00 a.m.). What are the next three times on a 24-hour clock?

## Common traps

- **Answering a middle step.** If the question asks what's left, the amount used is not the answer.
- **Ignoring units.** Convert minutes to hours (or the reverse) before multiplying by a rate.
- **Rounding down when you need to round up** (vans, boxes, doses to cover a full course).
- **Using every number.** Some numbers are distractors.
