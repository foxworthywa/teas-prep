# Estimation and Rounding

**TEAS objective M.1.7** · Number and Algebra

**What the TEAS expects:** you can round to a given place value, estimate answers to real-world problems, and decide when to round up or down in context.


**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Estimating first is the best check on a calculator answer.

---

## 1. Rounding rules

1. Find the place you are rounding to.
2. Look at the digit **just to its right**.
3. **5 or more → round up.** 4 or less → leave it.
4. Drop (or zero out) everything to the right.

| Number | Round to | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 4,672 | Nearest hundred | **4,700** |
| 3.4762 | Nearest hundredth | **3.48** |
| 12.35 | Nearest tenth | **12.4** |
| 0.0849 | Nearest hundredth | **0.08** |

**Place names:** in 3.4762, the 4 is tenths, 7 hundredths, 6 thousandths, 2 ten-thousandths.

> **Check 1:** Round 18.947 to the nearest tenth.

## 2. Estimating

Round each number to something easy, then compute.
- 49.8 × 6.1 ≈ 50 × 6 = **300** (exact: 303.78)
- 812 ÷ 39 ≈ 800 ÷ 40 = **20** (exact: about 20.8)
- $4.89 + $12.15 + $7.98 ≈ $5 + $12 + $8 = **$25** (exact: $25.02)

**Use estimates to check calculator answers.** If 49.8 × 6.1 comes out 3,037.8 on your calculator, the estimate (about 300) tells you the decimal point slipped.

---

## 3. Rounding in context

The situation decides the direction, not just the rule:
- **Round up** when you need **enough**: buses for students, boxes for items, paint cans for a wall. 74 students ÷ 12 per van = 6.17 → **7 vans**.
- **Round down** when you can only use **complete** units: how many $3 items can you buy with $20? 20 ÷ 3 = 6.67 → **6 items**.
- **Medication volumes** are rounded to what the measuring device can show: a syringe marked in tenths of a mL means rounding to the nearest tenth.

> **Check 2:** Tiles come in boxes of 12. A floor needs 130 tiles. How many boxes must you buy?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

## Common traps

- **Looking at the wrong digit.** Look at the digit just right of the place you're rounding to.
- **Rounding step by step.** 3.446 to the nearest tenth is 3.4; don't round to 3.45 first and then to 3.5.
- **Always following the 5-up rule in context.** "How many vans?" always rounds up.
- **Confusing tenths and tens.** Tenths are after the decimal point.
