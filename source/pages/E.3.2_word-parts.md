# Word Parts: Prefixes, Roots, and Suffixes

**TEAS objective E.3.2** · Using Language and Vocabulary to Express Ideas in Writing

**What the TEAS expects:** you can figure out what an unfamiliar word means by breaking it into its prefix, root, and suffix. Many TEAS examples come from medical vocabulary.

---

## 1. How word parts work

**prefix + root + suffix**: *hypo* (below) + *glyc* (sugar) + *emia* (blood condition) = **hypoglycemia**, low blood sugar.

A **prefix** comes at the beginning, the **root** carries the core meaning, and a **suffix** comes at the end. Read the **suffix first**, then the prefix, then the root: *gastritis* = inflammation (-itis) of the stomach (gastr).

**About the "/o":** roots are often written with a slash and an *o* (cardi/o). The *o* is a connecting vowel, used when the next part starts with a consonant (*cardiology*) and dropped before a vowel (*carditis*). It stays between two roots, even before a vowel: *gastr**o**enterology*.

## 5. Everyday word parts

Non-medical words work the same way:
- **Prefixes:** *un*happy, *dis*agree, *in*correct, *im*possible (all mean not); *re*write (again); *mis*spell (wrongly); *bi*cycle (two); *poly*gon (many).
- **Roots:** *port* = carry (transport, portable); *spect* = look (inspect, spectator); *bio* = life (biology).
- **Suffixes:** read*able* (able to be); care*less* (without); kind*ness* (state of).

---

## Common traps

- **Confusing hyper- and hypo-.** Hyper = over; hypo = under.
- **Confusing -ectomy, -otomy, and -ostomy.** Removal, cutting into, making an opening.
- **Ignoring the suffix,** which often carries the key meaning.
