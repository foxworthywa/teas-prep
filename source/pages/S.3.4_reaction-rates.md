# What Affects How Fast a Reaction Goes

**TEAS objective S.3.4** · Chemistry

**What the TEAS expects:** you can explain, using collision theory, how temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts change the rate of a reaction.

---

## 1. Collision theory

For a reaction to happen, particles must **collide** with **enough energy** (at least the **activation energy**, the energy needed to get a reaction started) and in the **right orientation**. Anything that makes successful collisions **more frequent** or **more energetic** speeds up the reaction.

---

## 2. The factors

| Factor | Change | Effect on rate | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Temperature** | Higher | **Faster** | Particles move faster: more collisions, and more of them have enough energy |
| **Concentration** (or gas **pressure**) | Higher | **Faster** | More particles in the same space collide more often |
| **Surface area** | Larger (powder vs a lump) | **Faster** | More particles exposed to collide |
| **Catalyst** | Added | **Faster** | Lowers the activation energy; not used up |
| **Stirring or mixing** | More | Faster | Brings reactants into contact |
| **pH** (enzyme reactions) | Near the enzyme's optimum | Fastest at the optimum | Too acidic or too basic changes the enzyme's shape |
| **Nature of reactants** | | Varies | Some substances are simply more reactive (sodium vs gold) |

**Everyday examples:**
- Food keeps longer in a **refrigerator** (lower temperature slows spoilage reactions).
- **Crushed** sugar dissolves and burns faster than a sugar cube (more surface area).
- A wood **fire** burns faster when you blow air on it (more oxygen = higher concentration).
- **Enzymes** are the body's catalysts; they let reactions run fast at 37 °C.

**Exception for enzymes:** heat speeds up most reactions, but too much heat (or the wrong pH) **denatures** an enzyme: its shape changes and the enzyme-driven reaction **slows or stops**. That's why enzyme activity plotted against temperature rises to a peak and then falls.

## 3. More about catalysts

- A catalyst provides an easier pathway with a **lower activation energy**.
- It is **not consumed**, so a small amount can be reused many times.
- It **speeds up** the reaction but does **not** change how much product can be made in the end.
- **Inhibitors** do the opposite: they slow a reaction.

---

## 4. Equilibrium (the basic idea)

Many reactions are **reversible** (⇌). At **equilibrium**, the forward and reverse reactions happen at the **same rate**, so the amounts of reactants and products stop changing, even though both reactions continue.

**Le Chatelier's principle:** if you disturb a system at equilibrium, it shifts to **counteract** the change. Add more reactant → it makes more product. Remove product → it makes more product to replace it.

## Common traps

- **Saying a catalyst is used up.** It is recovered unchanged.
- **Saying a catalyst raises the temperature or adds energy.** It lowers the energy barrier.
- **Thinking lower temperature stops reactions.** It slows them.
- **Thinking reactions stop at equilibrium.** Both directions continue at equal rates.
