# Spelling

**TEAS objective E.1.1** · Conventions of Standard English

**What the TEAS expects:** you can spot the correctly spelled word, choose between commonly confused words (their/there/they're, affect/effect), and apply common spelling rules.

---

## 1. Rules that cover most words

A **suffix** is an ending added to a word (*-ing, -ed, -ful, -ness*); a **prefix** is a beginning added to a word (*un-, re-*). Many spelling rules are about what happens when you add a suffix.

| Rule | Examples | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **i before e, except after c** (or when it sounds like "ay") | believe, field; receive, ceiling; neighbor, weigh | Exceptions: weird, seize, either, neither, foreign |
| **Drop the silent e** before a suffix that starts with a vowel | hope → hoping; care → caring | Keep the e before a consonant suffix: hopeful, careless |
| **Change y to i** when a consonant comes before the y | happy → happiness; carry → carried | Keep the y before -ing (carrying) or after a vowel (played) |
| **Double the final consonant** before a vowel suffix when the word ends in one vowel + one consonant **and** the last syllable is stressed (all one-syllable words count) | stop → stopped; run → running; occur → occurrence; begin → beginning | Don't double if the stress isn't on the last syllable (benefit → benefited, open → opened) or for two vowels (rain → raining), or for words ending in w, x, or y (fix → fixing, snow → snowing) |
| **Plurals:** add -es after s, x, z, ch, sh | boxes, churches, wishes | Consonant + y → -ies (baby → babies) |
| **Irregular (often Latin or Greek) plurals** | diagnosis → diagnoses; bacterium → bacteria; vertebra → vertebrae; nucleus → nuclei | Learn these by sight; they're common in health courses |

## 2. Commonly confused words

| Words | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| **their / there / they're** | their = belongs to them; there = a place; they're = they are |
| **your / you're** | your = belongs to you; you're = you are |
| **its / it's** | its = belongs to it; it's = it is |
| **affect / effect** | **A**ffect is usually the **A**ction (a verb: to influence); **E**ffect is usually the **E**nd result (a noun). Test: after *a, an, the*, or an adjective, use **effect** |
| **then / than** | then = time; than = comparison |
| **accept / except** | accept = receive or agree to; except = but, excluding |
| **to / too / two** | to = toward (go to class) or before a verb (to study); too = also or excessive; two = 2 |
| **whose / who's** | whose = belongs to whom; who's = who is |
| **lose / loose** | lose = misplace or fail to win; loose = not tight |
| **breath / breathe** | breath = noun (take a breath); breathe = verb (breathe deeply) |

**Test for contractions:** replace the word with the two words it stands for. "It's raining" → "It is raining" ✓. "The dog wagged it's tail" → "it is tail" ✗, so use **its**.

## 3. Words often misspelled

**accommodate** (two c's, two m's) · **necessary** (one c, two s's) · **separate** ("a rat" in separate) · **definitely** · **occurrence** (two r's) · **privilege** · **receive** · **schedule** · **benefited** · **embarrass** (two r's, two s's)

## Common traps

- **Trusting the "sound" of a word.** Many errors come from spelling words the way they're pronounced.
- **Putting an apostrophe in "its" for possession.** Possessive pronouns (its, yours, theirs) never take apostrophes.
- **Using "then" in comparisons.** "Taller than," not "taller then."
