# Building a Well-Organized Paragraph

**TEAS objective E.2.3** · Knowledge of Language

**What the TEAS expects:** you can pick a strong topic sentence, put sentences in a logical order, choose transitions, and spot sentences that don't belong.

---

## 1. Parts of a paragraph

1. **Topic sentence:** states the main idea, usually first.
2. **Supporting sentences:** details, examples, evidence, and explanation.
3. **Concluding sentence:** wraps up or leads to the next paragraph.

A good paragraph has **unity** (every sentence supports the main idea) and **coherence** (sentences flow in a logical order with clear transitions).

> **Check 1:** Which is the stronger topic sentence for a paragraph on sleep and studying: "I like to sleep in on Saturdays" or "Getting enough sleep improves memory and test performance"?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

## 2. Ways to order ideas

| Order | Use it for | Signal words |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronological / process** | Steps, events, procedures | first, next, then, finally |
| **Order of importance** | Arguments, reasons | most important, above all |
| **Spatial** | Describing a place or object | above, next to, on the left |
| **Cause and effect** | Explaining why something happens | because, as a result, therefore |
| **Compare and contrast** | Showing similarities and differences | similarly, however, in contrast |

---

## 3. Transitions

| Purpose | Transitions |
|---|---|
| Add | also, in addition, furthermore |
| Contrast | however, on the other hand, in contrast, nevertheless |
| Cause / result | therefore, as a result, consequently |
| Example | for example, for instance |
| Sequence | first, next, then, finally |
| Conclude | in conclusion, overall |

**Pick the transition by the relationship** between the two sentences, not by how it sounds.

**Punctuation:** a transition that starts a sentence is followed by a comma (*However, practice helps.*). To join two complete sentences with one, use a semicolon before it and a comma after (*Math worries many students; however, practice helps.*). **"Although" is different:** it starts a dependent clause, so it never stands alone with a comma ("Although, practice helps" ✗). Use it like *because*: *Although math worries many students, practice helps.*

> **Check 2:** Fill the blank: "Many students fear the math section. ___, regular practice builds confidence." (Therefore / However / For example)
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

## 4. Finding the sentence that doesn't belong

Ask of each sentence: does it **support the topic sentence**? A sentence can be true and interesting and still be off topic.

---

## Common traps

- **Choosing a detail or example as the topic sentence.**
- **Choosing a transition that signals the wrong relationship.**
- **Keeping an interesting but off-topic sentence.**
