# Mean, Median, Mode and Range

**TEAS objective M.2.2** · Measurement and Data

**What the TEAS expects:** you can calculate the mean, median, mode and range of a data set, find a missing value from a given mean, and judge which measure best describes data with outliers.


**Calculator:** the TEAS gives you a basic on-screen calculator. Use it to add long lists of values.

---

## 1. The four measures

Data set: **3, 5, 5, 8, 9**

| Measure | How to find it | Here |
|---|---|---|
| **Mean** (average) | Add all values, divide by how many | 30 ÷ 5 = **6** |
| **Median** | Put in order; take the **middle** value | **5** |
| **Mode** | The value that appears **most often** | **5** |
| **Range** | Largest − smallest | 9 − 3 = **6** |

**Even number of values:** the median is the **average of the two middle values**. For 2, 4, 7, 10 → (4 + 7) ÷ 2 = **5.5**.

A data set can have **no mode** (all values different) or **more than one** mode.

**Memory hook:** **MO**de = **MO**st; **MED**ian = **MID**dle (like the median of a highway).

## 2. Finding a missing value

**Total = mean × number of values.**

**Example:** a student's first four test scores are 82, 75, 90 and 88. What score on the fifth test gives an average of 85?
- Needed total: 85 × 5 = 425
- Current total: 82 + 75 + 90 + 88 = 335
- Needed score: 425 − 335 = **90**

---

## 3. Mean from a frequency table

| Score | Number of students |
|---|---|
| 70 | 2 |
| 80 | 5 |
| 90 | 3 |

Multiply each value by its count and add: 70 × 2 + 80 × 5 + 90 × 3 = 140 + 400 + 270 = 810. Divide by the total count (2 + 5 + 3 = 10): mean = **81**.

---

## 4. Outliers

An **outlier** is a value far from the rest. It pulls the **mean** toward it but barely moves the **median**.

**Example:** salaries $40k, $42k, $45k, $47k, $300k.
- Mean = $474k ÷ 5 = **$94.8k** (higher than four of the five salaries)
- Median = **$45k** (a better picture of the typical salary)

**Rule of thumb:** with outliers or skewed data, the **median** is the better "typical" value.

## Common traps

- **Finding the median without ordering the data first.**
- **Forgetting to average the two middle values** when there is an even count.
- **Confusing range with the largest value.** Range is a difference.
- **Using the mean for skewed data.** An outlier distorts it.
