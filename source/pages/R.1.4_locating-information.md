# Locating Specific Information

**TEAS objective R.1.4** · Key Ideas and Details

**What the TEAS expects:** you can quickly find a specific fact, such as a date, a place, a number, or a name, in a passage, memo, or schedule.

---

## 1. Scan, don't reread

**Scanning** means moving your eyes quickly to find one piece of information without reading every word.

1. Decide **what kind of answer** you need: a date? a room number? a price?
2. Pick a **key word** from the question to look for.
3. Scan for the key word **or** for the shape of the answer (numbers, capital letters, dollar signs).
4. When you find it, **read the full sentence** around it to confirm.

---

## 2. Watch for near-misses

Passages often include **several similar facts**: two dates, three rooms, a deadline, and a start date. Wrong answers are usually real details from the passage that answer a **different** question.

**Example:** *Submit the form by March 1. Permits will be mailed starting March 5.*
"When is the form due?" → **March 1** (not March 5).

> **Check 1:** *The library opens at 8 a.m. on weekdays and 10 a.m. on Saturday. It closes at 9 p.m. Monday through Thursday and at 5 p.m. on Friday and Saturday.* When does the library close on Friday?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

## 3. Use the text's structure

- **Headings and bold words** tell you where to look.
- **Lists and tables** line up facts; read across the correct row.
- **Memos** usually put who, what, and when in the first lines and exceptions near the end.

> **Check 2:** A memo says permits are picked up in Room 104 but forms are submitted online. A question asks where to pick up a permit. Which detail is the trap?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

## Common traps

- **Grabbing the first number you see.**
- **Mixing up a deadline with a start date.**
- **Not confirming** the answer by reading the whole sentence.
