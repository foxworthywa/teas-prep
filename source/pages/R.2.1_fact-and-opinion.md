# Fact, Opinion, Bias, and Misconceptions

**TEAS objective R.2.1** · Craft and Structure

**What the TEAS expects:** you can tell facts from opinions and spot bias, stereotypes, and misconceptions in a text.

A **fact** can be checked. An **opinion** is a belief or judgment. **Bias** is a one-sided slant.

---

## 1. Fact or opinion?

| Fact | Opinion |
|---|---|
| Can be **checked or measured** | Expresses a **judgment, belief, or feeling** |
| *A 20-ounce soda has about 16 teaspoons of sugar.* | *Selling soda in a hospital is ridiculous.* |
| *The clinic opens at 8 a.m.* | *The clinic should open earlier.* |

**Words that often signal opinion:** *should, best, worst, amazing, terrible, ridiculous, I believe.* They are clues, not proof; read the whole statement. ("Patients must fast for 12 hours" is a rule that can be checked.)

A statement of fact can be checked, and checking might show it is false. "The moon is 50 miles away" is a factual claim; checking shows it is wrong.

> **Check 1:** Fact or opinion? (a) "The flu shot is offered every fall." (b) "Everyone who skips a flu shot is careless."
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

## 2. Spotting bias

**Bias** is a one-sided slant that ignores or misrepresents other views. Watch for:

- **Overgeneralizations:** *every, all, always, never* applied to people ("Every administrator only cares about money").
- **Loaded words** that carry strong feelings (*greedy, heroic, disaster*).
- **Missing evidence:** big claims with no support.
- **Who benefits:** a company praising its own product.

---

## 3. Stereotypes

A **stereotype** is a fixed, oversimplified belief about everyone in a group: "Older patients can't learn new technology," or "Teenagers are lazy." Stereotypes ignore differences between individuals, so they are a form of bias. On the TEAS, a choice that judges a whole group by one trait is usually the biased one.

> **Check 2:** Which statement is a stereotype? (a) "Some of our patients prefer printed instructions." (b) "Older people never understand online forms."
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

## 4. Misconceptions

A **misconception** is a common but mistaken belief. Texts, especially ads, sometimes rely on one:

- "Natural means safe." (Many natural substances are harmful.)
- "If it worked for one person, it will work for me." (One story isn't evidence.)
- "More is better." (Doses have limits.)

> **Check 3:** An ad says, "Our tea is all natural, so there are no side effects." What misconception does it rely on?
> *Answer at the bottom of the page.*

---

## Common traps

- **Calling a statement an opinion because you disagree with it.**
- **Calling a statement a fact just because it includes a number.** Ask whether it can actually be checked and where the number came from.
- **Missing quiet bias** in word choice.

3. **That "natural" means "safe."** Natural products can still cause side effects.
